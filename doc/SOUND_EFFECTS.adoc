[[overview]]
== Overview

Every cue has a **synthesized fallback** — oscillator beeps built at play time —
so the game makes a sound even with nothing on disk. Twenty cues also ship a
**recording**, which takes over from the notes the moment its row exists in
`CUE_FILES` (`src/utils/Sfx.ts`). The other twenty-one are synthesized only —
the whole "news" row in the catalogue below, plus five early cues.

A recording replaces the synthesis *one cue at a time*. Filling in a row makes
that one cue play the file instead; every other cue keeps its oscillators.
Nothing else in the game changes, so a recording can land in a single commit
and be reverted the same way.

Three rules hold for both paths:

* **The switch comes first.** When `enableSoundEffects` is off, `playSfx`
  returns before it looks up a file — a muted game never even fetches the
  asset.
* **A cue is either a file or a synthesis, never both.** A path that failed to
  load does not fall back to the oscillators, so a half-configured game cannot
  double up when the recording finally arrives.
* **One voice, one purpose.** Every `playSfx` call goes through the
  SoundEngine (`src/utils/SoundEngine.ts`), which enforces cooldowns, plays
  at most one sound at a time, and picks the recording — see <<policy,Policy>>.
  And every recording serves exactly one cue: no file means two things.

=== The news cues

Ten cues for the moments that decide a game without a single shot being fired.
An audit of all 71 routed events found these were the big ones played in total
silence — see <<immersion-gaps,the gaps that were left>>. Every one is
synthesized: they cost no bytes and need no asset, so the game stays audible on
day one and a recording can take one over later exactly like any other cue.

[cols="1,4,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Cue | When it plays | Family | File

| `warDeclared`
| The player is dragged into a war — as aggressor or as target. Drums, not a
  melody: two low thumps with a noise slap, so it never reads as the espionage
  alarm. A war between two rivals is silent.
| event
| —

| `peaceMade`
| Hostilities the player is party to end — a peace treaty *or* a ceasefire. One
  voice for both, because "the fighting stopped" is one event in two costumes.
  A gentle major cadence that comes to rest, the opposite shape to `warDeclared`.
| event
| —

| `wonderCompleted`
| The player finishes a **Wonder** — the biggest single thing in a game, and it
  fires once. A slow five-step rise to a held twelfth. A rival's Wonder is
  silent.
| event
| —

| `cityLost`
| The player loses a city to a rival. A fall in a minor with the last note left
  hanging. See the catalogue row above.
| event
| —

| `civEliminated`
| A civilisation is wiped out — the player's own (the last note the game
  plays), or one the player destroyed. A bell toll: one deep strike with a
  dissonant partial over it. Two rivals wiping each other out is silent.
| event
| —

| `cityStarved`
| One of the **player's** cities starves and is razed. Thin, and it dies away
  rather than resolving. A famine elsewhere is silent.
| event
| —

| `cityDisorder`
| One of the **player's** cities tips into unrest. An agitated rise that turns
  on itself. See <<news-cue-gates,the gate>> for why only the onset sounds.
| event
| —

| `unitProduced`
| A unit rolls out of one of the player's cities, built *or* bought. Three even
  stamps rather than an arch, so it is neither `barracksBuilt` nor
  `productionComplete` and does not steal the electrification whistle. A
  caravan is excluded — it is built, not manufactured, and has its own cue.
| event
| —

| `tradeRouteEstablished`
| One of the **player's** caravans opens a route. A rising third and fifth,
  soft, over a wide city hum — commerce, not conquest.
| event
| —

| `unitPromoted`
| One of the player's units earns its next rank. The only news cue in the
  `unit` family, and the quietest of all ten: a promotion usually happens
  mid-battle, so it must sit under the fanfares rather than compete.
| unit
| —

[[news-cue-gates]]
=== What keeps the news cues quiet

A news cue is the easiest thing in the game to make irritating, because the
events behind them fire constantly. The AI declares wars on itself, promotes
units through every long war, starves cities, opens trade routes and finishes
Wonders all game long — and each of those events is something the player did
not do and cannot act on.

So all ten go through one helper, `playNewsCue` (`EngineEventHandlers.ts`), and
it enforces three things:

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Rule | Why

| The event must involve the player's civilisation
| A war between two rivals, a rival's famine, an AI promotion: silent. This is
  the same rule `unitDisbanded` and the movement cues already follow — the
  AI's bookkeeping is not sound design.

| `isAIVsAI` silences all of them
| In a self-playing run nobody is there to hear anything. This check is not
  redundant: civilization 0 still carries the human player id even when the AI
  drives it, so the id test alone would let a whole spectator run sing.

| The player must be the one who gains or loses
| For `civEliminated` that means both directions the player is party to — their
  own civilisation ending *and* one they destroyed. A third party wiping out a
  fourth is nobody's news.
|===

Two of the ten have an extra, deliberate narrowing:

* **`cityDisorder` sounds on the onset only.** The event carries the city's
  *new* state, so it also fires when unrest *ends*. Relief getting the same
  alarm as the uprising would train the player to ignore the cue, which is the
  one thing an alarm must never do.
* **`unitProduced` skips caravans.** A caravan is built rather than
  manufactured, and announcing it twice — once as a unit, once as a route —
  would double up on the same event.

[[immersion-gaps]]
=== The gaps that were left, and one on purpose

The audit behind <<the news cues,the news cues>> covered all 71 routed events
against the 14 that made a noise. Ten of the silent ones were filled. These are
the ones that were not, kept here so the silence reads as a decision rather than
an oversight:

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Event | Why it stays silent

| `TECH_RESEARCHED`
| **On purpose.** Research completes every few turns for the whole game, and it
  is the one news moment frequent enough to become wallpaper. A fanfare for
  every invention is the single fastest way to make a player mute the game.
  It is the obvious candidate if a recording ever lands for it.

| `ALLIANCE_FORMED`, `ALLIANCE_BROKEN`, `EMBASSY_ESTABLISHED`
| Diplomacy bookkeeping. `warDeclared` and `peaceMade` already cover the two
  moments a war actually turns on.

| `VILLAGE_RESULT`, `FISH_DELIVERED`
| Routine, and a city grown from a village already plays `cityFounded`.

| `UNIT_SKIPPED`, `UNIT_FORTIFIED`, `UNIT_SLEPT`, `UNIT_WOKE`, `SELECT_QUEUE_UNIT`
| Direct player actions that already carry `uiSelect`, or that repeat far too
  often to deserve a cue of their own.
|===

[[city-capture-marches]]
=== Why two city-capture marches

`einmarsch stadt klassisch` and `einmarsch stadt modern` are the same moment at
two points in history, so the game picks between them with its own clock: the
game year (`GameEngine.currentYear`) against `MODERN_ERA_YEAR` (1900 AD) in
`EngineEventHandlers.ts`. Before it the classical march plays, from it the
modern one.

Both are *capture* cues. Losing a city plays neither — a victory march over
your own fall would be the wrong news twice over.

[[machine-gun-ownership]]
=== Who gets the machine gun

The recording behind `machineGunStrike` is Warzone's
`short_machine_gun_fire` — a burst of automatic fire. It used to be the
fallback for *every* land unit, so a legion, a catapult and a tank all fired a
machine gun none of them carried. The gate is now the narrowest one in the
game:

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Attacker | Cue

| Mech. Inf.
| `machineGunStrike` — the only unit with machine guns.

| Every other land unit — warrior, archer, phalanx, chariot, knights, legion,
  catapult, cavalry, musketeer, riflemen, cannon, artillery, **tank**
| `impactStrike` — a dull knock, synthesized.

| Submarine / surface warship / aircraft
| `torpedoAttack` / `navalStrike` / `airStrike` — decided by the platform,
  before the machine-gun flag is ever consulted.
|===

The flag lives on `unitAttackCue` as a fourth argument that *defaults to false*,
so a caller who forgets to pass it gets `impactStrike` — silence rather than
something untrue. It is also checked after the platform branches, so a
machine-gunned unit that is really a submarine still fires a torpedo.

A **tank** is the deliberate edge case: it has a coaxial machine gun in the
real world and could honestly claim this cue, but it gets `impactStrike` so that
the tank's own voice stays the rumble in `tankMove` plus one distinct blow.
`impactStrike` is synthesized, which also means a land unit added to the game
later inherits a sound for free instead of silence.

[[city-assault-gate]]
=== Who gets the city-attack cue

`cityAttacked` is a **gunpowder bombardment** — walls under fire — so it starts
with the Musketeer and covers what the gunpowder line leads to. It is not a
generic "something attacked something" cue:

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Attacker | Result

| Musketeer, Riflemen, Mech. Inf., Cannon, Artillery
| `cityAttacked` plays.

| Warrior, Archer, Phalanx, Chariot, Knights, Legion, Catapult, Cavalry
| Silent — a sword or a stone engine does not bombard walls.

| **Tank**, Fighter, Bomber, Cruiser, Destroyer, Battleship, Submarine
| Silent — the Tank has its own guns, and an attack run or a broadside is not
  an assault on the walls. Each still plays its own weapon cue on the combat
  round.
|===

The set lives in `cityAssaultCue` (`SoundEngine.ts`) as an explicit list, and it
**fails closed**: an unknown or missing attacker type returns false, so a unit
added later cannot inherit a city fanfare nobody reasoned about.

[[swapped-recording-names]]
=== Two recordings that were renamed into place

`cityFounded` and `mechInfMove` arrived under names that did not describe what
they turned out to be, so the rename in `public/assets/sfx/` is load-bearing
rather than cosmetic:

[cols="1,1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Arrived as | Shipped as | What it actually is

| `GoGoGo.ogg`
| `city-founded.opus`
| A shouted "Go! Go! Go!" — three short bursts with a vocal harmonic stack
  around 1.1/2.2/3.3 kHz. A cheer, which is what a city founded sounds like.

| `city_founded.opus`
| `mech-inf-move.opus`
| A wide-band machinery rumble — 1.86 s of broadband noise with strong low
  content and several heavy bursts, and no voice in it at all. A tracked

|===

Both files were copied verbatim — only the names changed, so the audio data is
bit-for-bit identical to what was supplied. Each is renamed after the cue it
actually serves, which is what the convention in
<<adding-a-recording,Adding a Recording>> asks for; the original names survive
in this table so the provenance is not lost.

Note also that these two are *project originals*, not third-party assets, so
they sit directly in `public/assets/sfx/` and are **not** covered by the GPLv2

[[pipeline]]
== How a Cue Fires

[source]
----
playSfx(cue)
  │
  ├─ settings.enableSoundEffects === false ──► return, no lookup, no fetch
  │
  └─ SoundEngine.requestSound(cue)            ──► played / queued /
                                                  dropped (see <<policy>>)
       │
       ├─ recording resolved ──► Audio(path) ──► return
       │                         (one voice: never overlapping, see below)
       │
       └─ no recording ──► AudioContext (lazily created)
                           ├─ none or suspended ──► resume() once, drop the cue
                           └─ running ──► oscillator per note in CUES[cue]
----

The context is created on first use and **cached in module state**. Browsers
refuse to start an `AudioContext` before a user gesture, so `unlockAudio()` is
called from `GameCanvas.handleMouseDown` — the first click on the board warms
the context up and the next cue is not silently swallowed.

`unlockAudio()` is safe to call often; it only resumes a suspended context.

[[policy]]
== Cooldowns, Serialization and Variants

`src/utils/SoundEngine.ts` is the only thing that decides whether a requested
cue makes a noise. Three mechanisms, in order:

. **Cooldowns.** A cue replays at most once per its family's window. The
   ledger is per cue, not global — a blow does not mute a click.
+
[cols="1,1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Family | Window | Cues

| selection
| 0.3 s
| `unitSelected`, `submarineSelected`, `artillerieSelected`,
  `archerSelected`, `unitReSelected`. The shortest window in the game on
  purpose: a unit's acknowledgement is gated per UNIT, not per sound (see
  <<_selection_a_unit_answers_once_then_grunts>>), so this family only stops a
  single click from producing a machine-gun of blips. It must stay far below
  the repeat window or selecting unit A would silence unit B again.

| unit
| 10 s
| `tankMove`, `mechInfMove`, `airUnitLaunch` — everything a unit says on its
  own besides answering a click. A three-tile drive and a column of armour
  stay quiet.

| attack
| 4 s
| `machineGunStrike`, `impactStrike`, `navalStrike`, `airStrike`,
  `torpedoAttack`, `missileLaunch`, `nuclearStrike`, `unitDefeated` — every
  weapon. Blows land round after round, and each one must be heard without
  machine-gunning.

| event
| 2.5 s
| Everything else one-off: captures, sabotage, production, fanfares.

| ui
| 0.4 s
| `uiSelect` — fast double-orders must not stutter.
|===

. **Serialization.** The game has exactly one voice. While a cue is still
   ringing — for its full file duration, measured off the asset — the next
   one waits in a priority FIFO three deep: events first, then attacks, then
   selection acknowledgements, then unit sounds, then UI. A full line drops
   its least important waiter, and a unit answering a click outranks the
   movement chatter behind it (a fanfare bumps a click; a click never bumps a
   fanfare). Two sounds never overlap: a torpedo never steps on a fanfare, and
   the nuclear whistle always finishes before the boom starts.

. **Variants.** A cue with several recordings plays its MAIN file most of
   the time (weight 70 of 100) and a secondary the rest — the same blow does
   not sound byte-identical every round, but it stays recognisable. The
   manifest (`SOUND_MANIFEST`) lists every variant with its weight; the
   `CUE_FILES` row always points at the main file, and a runtime row that
   *differs* from it wins outright, so live repointing keeps working.

=== Selection: a unit answers once, then grunts

Selection is the one sound in the game the player triggers *deliberately*, and
it is a conversation with a particular unit rather than an event in the world.
It is therefore governed per **unit**, not per sound.

* First click on a unit — the full acknowledgement. A land unit reports in
  (`unitSelected`, or `archerSelected` / `artillerieSelected` in its own
  voice); a submarine answers with sonar (`submarineSelected`).
* Clicking the **same** unit again inside `SELECTION_REPEAT_WINDOW_MS` (4 s —
  the midpoint of the 3–5 s window) is the player re-issuing or cancelling an
  order, not a new introduction. It gets `unitReSelected`: one short downward
  blip, a quarter the length of the acknowledgement, that reads as "still
  here" rather than as a second "Yes, Commander?".
* Clicking it after the window is a fresh interaction and gets the full
  acknowledgement again. The ledger answers "did we *just* acknowledge this
  unit?", not "have we ever".
* Clicking a **different** unit always gets that unit's own acknowledgement.

This replaces a 10 s cooldown shared by every unit sound. That rule stopped
click-spam, but it was keyed on the *sound* rather than on the *unit*, so
picking up five units in a row produced one acknowledgement and four silences —
exactly backwards for a game where the player moves between units constantly.

One file, one purpose still holds: `unitReSelected` is a cue in its own right
serving the repeat click, and no recording is shared between it and the full
acknowledgements.

'''
A file holds the voice for its whole measured length — the durations in the
manifest were read off the assets with ffprobe, and
`tests/soundEngine.test.ts` fails if a hold is shorter than any of its
files. The legacy mp3 cues hold theirs too (`barracksBuilt` a full 6.75 s),
which is why they fire rarely and only for the player.

[[adding-a-recording]]
== Adding a Recording

. Drop the file in `public/assets/sfx/` — or in a vendor folder like
  `public/assets/sfx/warzone2100/` when the recording is third-party (see
  <<warzone-attribution,Warzone 2100 Attribution>>). **Name it after the cue
  in kebab-case** — the table above gives the name for each cue, and the cue
  `cityCapturedModern` is the file `city-captured-modern.mp3`. The name is a
  convention, not a lookup: only the path you write into `CUE_FILES` matters
  at runtime, and `tests/sfx.test.ts` asserts the convention holds.

. Edit `CUE_FILES` in `src/utils/Sfx.ts` and uncomment (or add) the row:
+
[source,typescript]
----
export const CUE_FILES: Partial<Record<SfxCue, string>> = {
  'cityCapturedClassic': '/assets/sfx/city-captured-classic.mp3',
  // ...
  // 'unitDefeated':     '/assets/sfx/unit-defeated.ogg',
};
----
+
The key is the cue id (`SfxCue`); the value is the URL **as the browser
requests it**. Vite serves everything under `public/` at the root, so the file
`public/assets/sfx/warzone2100/machine-gun-strike-1.ogg` is
`/assets/sfx/warzone2100/machine-gun-strike-1.ogg`.

. If the cue gets **several** recordings, add the secondaries to the
  `SOUND_MANIFEST` entry in `src/utils/SoundEngine.ts` — the `CUE_FILES` row
  stays the MAIN file, and the manifest holds the weights. The main file
  plays most of the time; a file that appears in two cues' lists breaks the
  one-purpose test.

. Measure the file's duration (ffprobe) and put it in the manifest's
  `durationSec` — the voice stays busy that long, and a shorter hold lets the
  next cue step on the tail.

. Reload. That cue now plays the file and its oscillators are ignored. All
  other cues are untouched.

Any format the browser can decode works (`Audio` just takes a URL). The shipped
recordings are `.mp3`, the placeholders above are written as `.ogg` because
that compresses well for the short percussive clips the remaining cues are —
neither is a lookup, so either will do.

A recording that is registered but missing from disk plays **nothing**: the
path *is* the lookup, and the synthesized fallback is deliberately not retried
so a half-configured game cannot double up. `tests/sfx.test.ts` walks the
shipped rows and asserts each file exists, which is what catches the typo.

[NOTE]
====
`CUE_FILES` is exported and read **live** on every call, so a row added after
the module loaded takes effect immediately — a test, or a future manifest
loader, can register a path without editing this module. A runtime row that
differs from a manifest cue's shipped main file wins outright; the manifest
only drives the shipped assignment.
====

[[gating]]
== When Sound Is Suppressed

Beyond the policy holds (<<policy,Policy>>), these gates keep specific cues
quiet. The rule behind all of them is the same: the AI's bookkeeping is not
sound design.

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Gate | Effect

| `settings.enableSoundEffects` (off)
| `playSfx` returns before any lookup or fetch. Toggled in Settings →
  *Enable sound effects*; the label exists in all six languages.

| No `AudioContext` (node, blocked audio)
| Synthesis is skipped. File cues still attempt `Audio(path)`, which fails
  quietly.

| Context still `suspended` (no gesture yet)
| The cue is **dropped, not queued** — a queue would replay stale combat when
  the context finally starts. `unlockAudio()` on the first click makes this
  rare.

| Cooldown (selection 0.3 s, unit 10 s, attack 4 s, event 2.5 s, UI 0.4 s)
| A re-fired cue is dropped. A three-tile drive rumbles once; a five-round
  battle knocks four times, not forty. Selection is the exception that proves
  the rule: its window is barely there, because re-selection is governed per
  unit (a grunt), not by silence.

| Re-selecting the same unit within 4 s
| The full acknowledgement is replaced by `unitReSelected`, a bare grunt — or
  nothing at all, when the queue is busy. See
  <<_selection_a_unit_answers_once_then_grunts>>.

| Voice busy, queue full
| The lowest-priority waiter is dropped. A fanfare bumps a click; a click
  never bumps a fanfare.

| Combat not involving the player
| `machineGunStrike` / `impactStrike` / `navalStrike` / `airStrike` /
  `unitDefeated` do not fire. The same rule as the toasts: two AI armies
  clashing off-screen must not fill a quiet turn, and an AI-vs-AI spectator run
  would otherwise play every round of every war.

| Disband by an AI civ
| `unitDisbanded` does not fire. The AI disbands constantly, and its
  bookkeeping is not sound design.

| An AI unit moving
| `tankMove` / `mechInfMove` / `airUnitLaunch` do not fire. The same rule as the
  disband: an AI turn is dozens of moves, and its bookkeeping is not sound
  design.

| A rival **founding** a city
| `cityFounded` does not fire. In an AI-vs-AI run nobody is the player, so it
  is silent there too.

| A rival's war, famine, Wonder, promotion, unit or caravan
| None of the ten news cues fire. Same rule as `unitDisbanded`: the AI does all
  of these constantly and its bookkeeping is not sound design. See
  <<news-cue-gates,the gate>>.

| Unrest **ending** in a player's city
| `cityDisorder` does not fire — the event carries the new state, so it also
  fires on the way out. Only the onset is an alarm.

| Selecting a unit that is not the player's
| `unitSelected` / `submarineSelected` do not fire — an enemy under the
  cursor is information, not a command.

| An AI-vs-AI siege
| `cityAttacked` does not fire — neither the city nor the attacker is the
  player's.

| An AI city finishing anything
| `barracksBuilt` / `productionComplete` do not fire; the cues are the
  player's own feedback.

| The player **losing** a city
| Neither `cityCapturedClassic` nor `cityCapturedModern` fires (see the
  catalogue).
|===

[[testing]]
== Tests

`tests/sfx.test.ts` covers the transport:

* a row in `CUE_FILES` produces an `Audio` for exactly that path;
* the table is read **live**, so registering a path after import still applies;
* an unmapped cue never reaches the file layer (no double playback);
* sound off means no fetch at all;
* every cue in `SfxCue` plays without throwing, muted or not;
* a unit cue is quieter than an event fanfare, and two fanfares are equally
  loud;
* every shipped row has a real file on disk, named after its cue in kebab-case;
* the mutation tests put the table back exactly as they found it — the
  baseline is the shipped rows, not an empty table;
* every test starts from a cold engine (`resetSoundPolicy`), so no
  file's cooldowns leak into another's.

`tests/soundEngine.test.ts` covers the policy, on a fake clock with a
recording backend:

* unit sounds hold 10 s, attack sounds 4 s, and cooldowns are per cue;
  selection acknowledgements are their own family (0.3 s) and are gated per
  UNIT, not per sound — see `selectionAckFor` / `playUnitSelection`;
* a re-click of an already-acknowledged unit within 4 s gets a grunt, a
  different unit always gets its own acknowledgement;
* a second cue queues behind the first and the line drains most important
  first; a full line drops its least important waiter;
* the main recording plays most of the time (weight 70), and a runtime row
  that differs from the shipped main wins outright;
* all sixteen Warzone files exist, each serves exactly one cue, the
  `CUE_FILES` row points at the main file, and every manifest hold covers
  its longest file;
* the hull-to-weapon mapping (`unitAttackCue`, `unitSelectCue`).

`tests/sfxWiring.test.ts` covers the other half: which event plays what. It
drives a real engine through the real router with the sound layer mocked, and
pins the rules that keep the recordings from becoming noise —

* a tank rumbles once per turn, not once per tile;
* mech. inf. grinds once per turn on its own cue, never the tank's;
* a city the player founds gets the cheer — from a settler or from a village,
  and a rival founding one stays silent;
* an aircraft plays its takeoff only when it leaves a city or a carrier;
* the AI's moves are silent;
* a submarine gets `torpedoAttack`, a warship `navalStrike`, an aircraft
  `airStrike`, Mech. Inf. gets `machineGunStrike`, and everyone else
  `impactStrike`;
* no unit without machine guns ever reaches `machineGunStrike`, and a decisive
  round does not promote the blow underneath `unitDefeated`;
* the city-attack cue fires for gunpowder infantry and artillery, and not for a
  tank, an aircraft, a ship or a pre-gunpowder attacker;
* each of the ten news cues fires for the player's own event and stays silent
  for a rival's — including the pair that share a voice (a ceasefire takes
  `peaceMade`);
* `cityDisorder` sounds on the onset of unrest and **not** when it ends, and
  `unitProduced` skips caravans;
* in an AI-vs-AI run every news cue is silent, even for civilization 0, which
  still carries the human player id;
* a nuclear strike plays the launch whistle and then the explosion;
* a city-capture fanfare splits on `MODERN_ERA_YEAR`, and losing a city plays
  neither march;
* a barracks speaks in the player's own city, and every other completion
  gets the works whistle.

`disposeAudio()` exists for these tests: the context is module state, so two
assertions in one file would otherwise share whichever fake the first one
installed. Nothing in the game calls it — a real context is cheap to keep and
expensive to recreate.

[[screen-flash]]
== The Other Half of the Boom

Sound alone does not sell a nuclear detonation, so the same two event handlers
drive a full-screen flourish: a white flash and a shake of the map. It lives in
`GameCanvas.VisualEffects` and `styles/civ1GameCanvas.css`, not in the sound
layer.

A `VisualEffect` on the store carries `col`, `row`, `duration` and `shake`, and
one is pushed per event by `EngineEventHandlers`. The renderer does nothing but
turn it into DOM nodes:

* the **flash** is a white layer fading to zero, with a slower radial bloom
  behind it, so the light reads as light rather than as a white rectangle;
* the **shake** is a CSS keyframe animation on the canvas root — `transform`
  only, so it is composited and costs no map repaint. Every keyframe carries a
  `scale(1.02)`, because the root is exactly the size of its container and a
  bare translation would bare the background at one edge;
* both are `forwards`, and the node removes **itself** on `animationend`. There
  is no rAF loop and no polling, so the whole feature costs nothing except
  while it is actually playing.

Three rules keep it from becoming annoying:

. **The same gate as the sound.** Only the player's own strike, a blast within
   6 tiles of their cities, or their own reactor meltdown. In an AI-vs-AI run
   nobody is the player, so nothing flashes.
. **A meltdown is a smaller fire.** `onNuclearMeltdown` uses 60 % of the
   duration and half the shake, so the two explosions are not mistaken for one
   another.
. **`enableAnimations` switches it off.** Players who turned animations off get
   the notification and the sound, not the light show.

`tests/sfxWiring.test.ts` covers the wiring: the flash appears when the player
fires, when the blast lands near home, and on their own meltdown; it stays away
when the detonation is somebody else's problem; and the effect is dropped when
the renderer is done with it.
