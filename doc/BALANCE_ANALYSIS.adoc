= Balance & Behaviour Analysis — Economy, AI and Map Generation

Analysis of the Civ1 clone's economic model and computer-player behaviour, based
on the code and on eight unattended 400-round games run headlessly.

== Method

Six reproducible AI-vs-AI games were driven through the real engine with no
browser (`scripts/run-ai-batch.mjs`), each to 400 rounds or until the game
ended — which, as it turns out, it never did:

[cols="1,1,1,1,1"]
|===
|Run |Map |Civs |Rounds reached |Civs alive at the end

|seed 7 |AI_VS_AI 40×40 |4 |401 |2 of 4
|seed 19 |AI_VS_AI 40×40 |4 |401 |3 of 4 (1 has 0 units)
|seed 33 |AI_VS_AI 40×40 |4 |400 |**0 of 4**
|seed 51 |AI_VS_AI 40×40 |4 |400 |**0 of 4**
|seed 7 |AI_VS_AI 40×40 |6 |401 |1 of 6
|seed 19 |AI_VS_AI 40×40 |6 |400 |6 of 6 (4 have 0 units)
|===

Data per game: per-civ cities/population/units/techs/gold every 5 rounds, rate
changes, buildings, production, event counts. A naval batch (2 × 400 rounds on
AI_VS_AI_NAVAL) was **killed after 82 CPU-minutes without finishing** — see
<<perf>>.

Raw data: `game-logs/` plus the batch JSON (not committed).

== How the economy actually works

Because several rules are transcribed in `doc/GAME_RULES.adoc` differently from
the code, the summary below is what the engine really does.

=== One city, one turn

Tile yields (terrain + resource + improvement) → worked-tile assignment →
`trade` is the only currency: `commerce = max(trade, 2)`. Commerce is then:

* reduced by corruption — `0.3` **hard-coded base**, scaled to `2.0` by
  Euclidean distance from the capital, halved by a Courthouse
  (`CityUtils.calculateCorruption`)
* reduced by a government's `commercePenalty` (only Communism, −25 %)
* split by the tax/science/luxury rates into gold / beakers / happiness,
  `tax × TRADE_GOLD_MULTIPLIER (2)`

Growth threshold is `(population + 1) × 10`; citizens eat 2 food; settlers eat 1
(2 under Republic/Democracy) and cost 1 shield under those governments. A
Granary keeps half the food box. There is **no** free unit support: every unit
pays its per-type maintenance (1–6 gold), buildings 1–4 (wonders 0).

=== What the model gets right

* City growth works. Cities reach pop 7–12 in the surviving games and run 2–4
  specialists; the tile assigner's food-floor rule keeps them fed.
* The AI's rate controller is stable: 16–34 rate changes per 400 rounds, no
  0↔100 oscillation.
* Research works: dead civs still finish 17–47 technologies, and games reach
  mid/late-game techs (riflemen, tanks, space flight).
* Economy buildings appear: palaces, granaries, temples, marketplaces,
  libraries, colosseums are built in sensible proportions.

=== What the model hides

* **Disbanding is the real story and it is invisible in the telemetry.** Every
  game logs hundreds of `UNIT_DISBANDED` events (419 in one 4-civ game, 196 in
  a 6-civ game) while the harness's economy counters report **zero** bankrupt
  turns and zero negative-gold rounds. The reason: the AI is forgiven back to
  the 8-gold floor the moment it dips below zero, and gold is sampled only
  every 5 rounds. The AI is running a permanent deficit and paying for it by
  dissolving its army — including, presumably, the garrisons that would stop the
  barbarians.
* **The government's corruption number is dead data.** `GOVERNMENTS[].corruptionRate`
  (0.05 democracy … 0.3 despotism) is never read; corruption is a flat 0.3 ×
  distance. `corruptionReduction` on marketplace, bank, palace and stock
  exchange is equally inert. Late-game government choice therefore changes only
  the tax cap and happiness bonus.

== What the AI actually does

The turn is: strategy → research → government → diplomacy → plans → army groups
→ buildings → units. Within that, decisions are individually reasonable:

* It keeps 1 defender per city (`getCityDefenseReserveIds`), forms up to 2 army
  groups of ≥3, needs 1.3× strength parity and ~7 units-equivalent for a city
  siege, and only declares a war it can walk to.
* It caps the army at what it can pay for, sells unprofitable buildings, and
  prefers settlers over soldiers when it is "broke but solvent".
* The naval doctrine budgets ships against real income and pressure.

The failures are all in *which* threat it chooses to answer, and in what it
counts as progress.

== Findings

=== F1. Barbarians snowball into the AI's collapse (severe)

`chooseWeakestCity` targets the single lowest-defence city in the world; the
horde needs only 2 units and 1.5× that defence. Every captured city is then
forced to `autoProduction = true`, has its buildings sold, and pumps raiders
**forever**.

Measured: 5–7 barbarian cities at the end of the collapsed games, and 16 of 28
civ-games ending with the civ owning nothing. Deaths cluster between rounds 65
and 235. Once the last city falls the civ can never recover — it has no city to
build a settler in — so it idles for the remaining 100–330 rounds, researching
techs and hoarding gold (median 278, max 3,269) forever.

The AI has no counter-priority for this: barbarians appear only in the generic
5-tile enemy scan ("attack only civs at war + barbarians"), while the offensive
plan and army groups chase *civ* targets across the map.

=== F2. The settler and scout treadmill (severe)

Cadence caps count **living** units. So every scout or settler that dies is
replaced, forever:

[cols="1,1"]
|===
|Cap |Produced across 28 civ-games

|scouts — `AI_ABSOLUTE_MAX_SCOUTS = 3` |**250** (106 in one game, 76 in another)
|settlers — target 3–5 per profile |**184**
|===

Each replacement costs food, shields and upkeep, and they die on the way: one
game logged 196 `settler_leaves_city` events and 465 oscillation skips. The
result is an economy that spends its production on churn instead of on the
buildings that would make cities defensible and happy.

=== F3. Settlers improve tiles that no city can use

Two games spent 41 and 54 improvement worker-turns and finished with
**zero** improved tiles within 4 tiles of any city. The works mandate is not
scoped to a city's radius, so settlers walk off into the wilderness.

=== F4. The AI's unit loop mostly fails

One 4-civ game logged 2,032 `skip:insufficient_moves`, 1,198
`move:no_path_fallback`, 914 `no_target` and 465 oscillation skips — i.e. the
majority of unit attempts in a turn end without a useful action, while a
100 ms start delay and per-unit loops burn the turn budget.

=== F5. Army groups declare far more attacks than they land

`bulk_attack` is logged 185 times in one game against 31 real `attack` events
and 8 `declare_war`. Group formation, rallying and readiness consume the turn,
and the "attack" is mostly intent.

=== F6. A game with no humans has no ending

`VictoryManager.evaluateEndOfTurn` is called from exactly one place:
`TurnManager.advanceTurn` (TurnManager.ts:687). The headless harness never
calls it, which is why every game above reports `over: false` — but it also
means an AI-vs-AI game that loses *all* its civs (2 of 4 standard games)
declares no winner at all, and in a no-human game the only possible conclusion
is the barbarians winning.

=== F7. Happiness buildings are capped for the AI only

`HAPPINESS_BUILDINGS_PER_CITY = 4` is enforced in `AIBuildingStrategy`; the
engine's `buildingHappiness` has no cap, so the human can stack temples. Same
rules must apply to both seats.

[[perf]]
=== F8. Naval AI turns do not converge

The naval batch (2 games × 400 rounds) burned **82 CPU-minutes** without
finishing, while the equivalent standard batch (4 games) needed ~50 minutes
wall-clock. `aiTurnTimeoutMs` is a `setTimeout` watchdog, and a `setTimeout`
cannot interrupt a synchronously running AI turn — so the watchdog cannot fire
while the very thing it is meant to stop is running.

== Recommendations — game rules

Ordered by expected effect on the measured problems.

=== R1. Give barbarians a ceiling (fixes F1)

* Cap barbarian-held cities at `min(2, civilizations − 1)`. Beyond the cap a
  captured city is razed, not converted; it must not become a permanent
  raider factory.
* Scale the assault gate with population: require 1.5× defence **and** at least
  one more barbarian unit than the city has garrison units, so a defended city
  is not free food.
* Make barbarian units decay (e.g. −1 health every 10 rounds) or stop spawning
  once the horde holds no city for N rounds, so the snowball has friction.

=== R2. Make the government's corruption number real (fixes "dead data")

Use `GOVERNMENTS[].corruptionRate` as the distance base (despotism 0.30 →
democracy 0.05) and honour `corruptionReduction` on marketplace/bank/palace/stock
exchange. Late-game governments then mean something beyond the tax cap, and
Republic/Democracy/Communism become playable instead of cosmetic. This also
gives the AI a real reason to research Code of Laws and to build Courthouses.

=== R3. Stop the treadmill (fixes F2)

Count units **produced** in a rolling window (e.g. 25 rounds) against the
cadence target in addition to living units, for settlers and scouts alike. A
civ that keeps replacing the same three scouts should stop buying scouts and
start buying walls.

=== R4. Scope public works to a city's radius (fixes F3)

Improvement orders must target tiles inside some own city's radius +2, and the
settler governor must score work by the food/production it actually feeds. A
settler that cannot improve a nearby tile should join a city or explore instead.

=== R5. Parity and endings (fixes F6, F7)

* Apply the happiness-building cap to human cities too, or delete it from the
  AI.
* Move the victory/defeat check onto the round boundary (`advanceYear`) so every
  path that ends a round evaluates it, and explicitly handle the no-human case:
  when no civilization is human, the last operational civ (barbarians included)
  wins and the game ends.

=== R6. Make distress visible (fixes "hidden bankruptcy")

Keep the 8-gold forgiveness, but count it: emit an `ECONOMY_FORGIVEN` event and
track `disbanded`, `floorForgiven` and `negativeGoldTurns` per civ in the
progression export. Today the CSV cannot distinguish a healthy civ from one
that is being liquidated.

== Recommendations — map generator

* **AI-vs-AI maps are too small for their civ count.** Peak cities per civ were
  1–4 on 40×40 with 4–6 civs, and every game ended in territorial collapse.
  Either raise the default scenario map to ~60×60 for 4 civs, or drop the
  scenario to 3 civs. Fewer, more survivable empires will produce longer, more
  interesting wars.
* **Place starts ≥12 tiles apart** and verify no start sits within 4 tiles of a
  bonus resource cluster owned by a neighbour; early border friction is what
  drives the AI into unwinnable early wars.
* **Grassland has no default resource**, and food only comes from grassland,
  river/lake tiles or Game / Oasis / Seal / Fish. Early food scarcity is what makes
  the settler food demand (2 under Republic/Democracy) lethal. Give each start
  one guaranteed food resource (Game or Oasis) within its central 5×5.
* **Rivers**: the 1-wide-fordable rule is good, but generated rivers frequently
  cut a city's radius in half without a ford on the border. Guarantee one
  fordable tile per river/city intersection.

== Recommendations — AI

=== A1. Barbarians become the priority target when a horde exists (fixes F1)

When any barbarian city exists within ~12 tiles of an own city, or any own city
has `netThreat > 0` from barbarians:

* reserve **2** defenders per threatened city (not 1),
* form a "hunt the horde" group from units with `attack ≥ 2`,
* suspend offensive plans against other civs until the horde is gone.

The offensive plan already cancels itself when a city has `netThreat ≥ 2.5` —
extend that to a hard veto on *other* wars while barbarians hold cities.

=== A2. Stop buying churn (fixes F2, F3)

* Setters: count works tasks **inside radii** only; if the works budget is zero,
  the settler target drops to the expansion target.
* Scouts: hard cap on produced-per-window (R3), and re-task a scout that has
  explored its zone to garrison duty instead of producing a replacement.
* Ships/ferries: the naval doctrine is well-budgeted; keep it, but stop the
  *land* army from being starved to feed it.

=== A3. Fix the unit loop (fixes F4, F5)

* Cache the passability/occupancy grid per turn so `move:no_path_fallback`
  stops re-running A* for the same blocked tile.
* Drop a group that has spent more than `RALLY_TIMEOUT_ROUNDS + 10` rounds
  without reaching readiness instead of re-planning it every turn.
* Log intent (bulk_attack) separately from outcomes (attack, CITY_CAPTURED) so
  the telemetry stops reporting 185 attacks for 31 events.

=== A4. Rebuild before disbanding (fixes hidden bankruptcy)

`economyBeforeArmy` fires only when `gold ≥ reserve`. Extend it: when the AI is
*about* to disband (`gold < 0` this turn), sell the least useful building
first — the machinery `runBuildingAnalysis` already has — and only then
disband, and never below one defender per city with a barbarian city nearby.

=== A5. Freeze dead civs (fixes wasted turns)

A civ with no cities and no units should be skipped entirely (no AI turn, no
log). Two of the six games spent hundreds of rounds processing civs that could
not act. This also makes the harness's numbers readable.

=== A6. Naval performance (fixes F8)

* Compute the naval doctrine verdict once per *round* per civ, not per
  city/queue slot (it is cached per civ, but the cache key is re-derived for
  every city).
* Give the AI a **turn budget in work units**, not wall-clock: stop the unit
  loop when `units processed × cost > budget`, and let the next turn continue.
  A `setTimeout` watchdog cannot interrupt synchronous work; only an internal
  budget can.

== Suggested order

. **R1 + A1** (barbarian ceiling and counter-priority) — the single highest-value
  change; it addresses the collapse that ends 5 of 6 games.
. **R3 + A2** (production churn caps) — cheap, and it frees the production that
  A1 needs.
. **R2** (real corruption) — makes the government tree and the AI's building
  choices meaningful.
. **R5 + A5** (endings and dead civs) — makes future measurements possible.
. **R4 + A3** (works scoping, unit loop) — quality of life.
. **A6 + R6** (naval budget, telemetry) — engineering.

Re-run `node scripts/run-ai-batch.mjs 4 400 4` after each step and compare
against the baseline in this document: 16/28 civ-games ending city-less, 250
scouts and 184 settlers produced, 0/6 games reaching a conclusion.
