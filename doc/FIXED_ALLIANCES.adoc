== 1. What a fixed alliance is

A *fixed alliance* is a game mode chosen at setup: the player divides the
civilizations into permanent teams before the first turn. From then on the team
is the unit of survival, war and victory:

* Teammates can never fight, spy on, bribe, embargo or betray each other.
* A war declared by or against one member is a war between the whole teams.
* Peace and ceasefires are signed between whole teams.
* Every member wins or loses with the team, including members already
  eliminated when the result is decided.
* A human whose own civilization has fallen but whose team still fights keeps
  watching as a spectator instead of losing immediately.

A team is **not** a new `DiplomaticStatus`. It is a locked layer *above* the
existing pair-based diplomacy. Ordinary alliances, treaties and opinions still
exist between civilizations on different teams, and between a team member and
an outside civilization. This keeps the existing diplomacy model intact and
makes free-for-all games behave exactly as before.

Fixed alliances are a setup-time mode. They cannot be switched on or off during
a game, and a team cannot be changed after the first turn.

== 2. Setup rules

The toggle lives in the setup modal under *Fixed Alliances*. It is disabled
below three civilizations, because a two-civ game has no third party to ally
against and every "team" would be a singleton.

A valid assignment must satisfy all of:

. One team index per civilization *slot* (`0..N-1`), not per `CIVILIZATIONS`
  entry. Slots are what the engine creates, and hotseat seats map to the first
  slots.
. At least two teams.
. At least one team with two or more civilizations.

Team indices are normalised to a dense `0..teamCount-1` range before they are
stored, so a hand-written assignment such as `[3, 7, 3, 7, 9]` becomes
`[0, 1, 0, 1, 2]`. Invalid assignments disable the mode and fall back to
free-for-all rather than throwing; the setup screen blocks *Start* while the
assignment is invalid.

The setup screen offers:

* *Humans vs AI* — every human seat on team 1, every AI on team 2.
* *N even teams* — slots distributed round-robin over 2, 3 or 4 teams.
* *Auto-balance* — re-spread the current number of teams evenly.
* A per-slot team selector, with live team sizes and a validation message.

The summary panel shows `teams · civilizations` before the game starts.

== 3. Engine model

`TeamManager` is the single source of truth. It owns:

* whether the mode is enabled,
* the civilization → team map,
* the team → members map (ascending slot order),
* validation and normalisation,
* team names (`Team 1`, `Team 2`, …),
* team survival (`isTeamAlive`, `getAliveTeamIds`).

`Civilization.teamId` is a *denormalised copy* written by
`TeamManager.applyToCivilizations()`. It exists for the UI and the save file;
all engine decisions call `TeamManager` instead of comparing `teamId` directly.

Key methods:

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
|Method |Meaning

|`enabled` / `teamCount`
|Whether this is a team game and how many teams exist.

|`getTeamId(civId)`
|Team index, or `null` when the civ has no team.

|`getTeamMembers(teamId)`
|All civilization slots in a team, ascending.

|`getTeamMembersOf(civId)`
|The civ itself plus its teammates. A lone civ expands to `[civId]`, so the
same expansion code works in free-for-all games.

|`getTeammates(civId)`
|Teammates only, never the civ itself.

|`isTeammate(civA, civB)`
|True only for two *distinct* civs on the same team.

|`isTeamAlive(teamId)` / `getAliveTeamIds()`
|A team is alive while at least one member has a living unit or a city.

|`getTeamName(teamId)`
|One-based display name (`Team 1`).

|`applyToCivilizations()`
|Writes `Civilization.teamId` for UI/save.

|`validateTeamAssignments(assignments, civCount)`
|Standalone validation shared by the engine and the setup screen.
|===

`TeamManager.configure()` runs after the civilizations are created and again on
load. `reset()` clears every map when a new game starts or a save is loaded.

== 4. Diplomacy

`DiplomacyManager.applyTeamInvariants()` is called after diplomacy
initialization and again after a save is restored. It is idempotent and runs
last on load, so even a hand-edited save containing a teammate war is repaired.

For every teammate pair it forces:

* `status = 'alliance'`,
* an embassy,
* contact (teammates always know each other),
* `open_borders`,
* no embargo against a teammate,
* zero war exhaustion,
* no stale `warWith` entries on either civilization.

=== Team-wide war, peace and ceasefire

`declareWar`, `makePeace` and `signCeasefire` expand to the cross-product of the
two teams. Attacking one member brings the whole defending team in; the
aggressor's team joins the attack. The global reputation hit and the
`WAR_DECLARED` event are emitted once per team war, not once per pair.

`getEnemyTeamCount(civId)` counts *distinct enemy teams*, not enemy
civilizations, so a three-civ coalition is one war front for the AI's war cap.
`estimateTeamStrength(civId)` sums the military strength of every teammate, so
an AI does not attack a three-civ alliance believing it faces one opponent.

=== What teammates may still do

Trade, gold gifts, knowledge exchange, technology sales and requests remain
available. The lock forbids betrayal, not commerce. The diplomacy screen shows
a locked-alliance callout and withholds every hostile or redundant term:

* no declare war / break alliance,
* no demand tribute, bribe, spy, sabotage, steal technology or incite revolt,
* no propose alliance, peace, ceasefire, open borders, mutual defence,
  non-aggression, joint war, call for help or embassy — these are already
  guaranteed by the lock.

Treaties that form part of the lock (`open_borders`, `mutual_defense`,
`embargo_target`) cannot be cancelled with a teammate.

=== Outside civilizations

Alliances and treaties with civilizations outside the team stay *per civ*.
There is no team-wide alliance with a third party. A team war overrides an
ordinary alliance: if a team member is allied with a civilization that the team
is now at war with, the alliance is broken and the war applies to the whole
team. Calls for help and offensive calls skip locked teammates, because they
join automatically through the team-wide war.

=== AI

AI diplomacy is teammate-aware at three layers:

* `AIDiplomacyPolicy` filters hostile and redundant candidates for a teammate.
* `DiplomatAbilities` offers a teammate only trade, gold and knowledge.
* `AIManager`, `AIUtility`, `EnemySearcher` and `AIUtility.findNearbyEnemy` treat
  teammates as friendly when scanning for targets, and use team strength for
  attack, tribute and bribery estimates.

An AI never starts a war inside its own team, even if a stale relation or a
future code path tries to.

== 5. Combat and movement

Locked teammates are friendly everywhere the engine asks "is this hostile?":

* `combatUnit()` refuses a teammate attacker/defender pair as its last line of
  defence, even if a stray order reaches it.
* Attack targeting, stacked-enemy detection, AI target selection and diplomat
  targeting skip teammates.
* Splash damage never hits a teammate.
* City capture and last-city rules never treat a teammate city as foreign.
* Unloading and city entry accept teammate cities; a teammate army does not
  project a hostile zone of control against settlers.
* SDI coverage ignores the firer's own team's batteries, and nuclear targeting
  refuses a teammate target; the blast also spares teammate units and cities.

Movement through teammate territory is ordinary friendly movement; there is no
extra shared-vision or shared-map rule in v1 (see § 9).

== 6. Victory, defeat and spectating

`VictoryManager` switches to team evaluation when `TeamManager.enabled` is true.

* *Team moonshot:* the first operational member to complete Moonshot wins for
  the whole team, whichever member built the parts.
* *Team elimination:* the last team with an operational member wins. A team
  with one surviving unit or one city is still alive.
* *Team defeat:* when the primary human's team has no operational member and no
  other human team is alive, the whole team loses.
* *Spectating:* if the human's own civilization is eliminated but a teammate
  survives, the game does not end. The human keeps watching until the team
  result; the dead civ is skipped by the turn order.
* *Hotseat:* the first human civilization's team is the primary perspective for
  the shared result screen. Every member of the winning or losing team is listed
  in `GameResult.civilizationIds` / `civNames`, with `teamId` and `teamName` set.

Team survival uses a strict operational check that ignores units already marked
`isDefeated` (they linger only for the death animation). Free-for-all games keep
their historical operational rule unchanged.

`GameResult` additions:

[cols="1,2", options="header"]
|===
|Field |Meaning

|`teamId?: number`
|The team that won or lost, when this is a team result.

|`teamName?: string`
|Display name (`Team 1`).

|`civilizationIds` / `civNames`
|Every member of that team, including members eliminated earlier.
|===

== 7. User interface

=== Setup

The setup modal owns the toggle, team count, presets, per-slot builder, live
sizes and validation described in § 2. `Start` is disabled while the assignment
is invalid. The chosen team count and assignments are written into
`GameSettings` only when the mode is valid.

=== In game

* *Top bar:* the active civilization's team badge, tinted with the shared team
  palette.
* *Side panel:* the same badge beside the player name.
* *Diplomacy modal:* a team roster in the civilization list (with `You` and
  `Fallen` markers), inline `T1`/`T2` badges on foreign rows, a team badge on the
  leader panel, and a locked-alliance callout with only trade/gold/knowledge
  terms. Treaty cancel buttons are hidden for teammates.
* *Statistics → Players:* a team badge beside each civilization name.
* *Result overlay:* a team roster section listing every member's cities,
  population, score and fallen status; the Act I/II titles and ledes use the
  team name; the final ledger aggregates the team's cities, population, wonders,
  technologies and score.

The palette and the one-based name helper live in `src/data/TeamConstants.ts`
(`TEAM_COLORS`, `teamColor`, `teamName`) so setup, badges and results never
disagree about a team's colour or label.

== 8. Save and load

The save format is *version 5*. The team state rides in two places:

* `gameSettings.fixedAlliances` — whether the mode is on,
* `gameSettings.teamAssignments` — slot-indexed team indices,
* `civilizations[].teamId` — the denormalised copy used by the UI.

On load the engine validates the version (`1..5`), restores the settings and
civilizations, rebuilds `TeamManager` from `teamAssignments`, then reapplies the
team invariants *after* the diplomacy relations are restored. A stale teammate
war in the save is therefore repaired to an alliance.

Versions 1–4 have no team fields and load as free-for-all. A version-5 save
with `fixedAlliances: false` (or an invalid assignment) also loads as
free-for-all.

See link:save-load-system.adoc[Save & Load System] for the full format.

== 9. Scope and deferred work

Shipped in v1:

* permanent membership and validation,
* team-wide diplomacy and war/peace,
* combat, AI and diplomat guards,
* team victory/defeat and human spectating,
* setup and in-game UI,
* save/load and tests.

Deliberately deferred to v1.1:

* *Shared team vision.* Teammates do **not** share fog of war or explored maps
  in v1. Each civilization keeps its own visibility and exploration.
* Team chat, shared research or shared gold.
* Team-level treaties with outside civilizations (alliances remain per civ).
* Reassigning teams during a game.
