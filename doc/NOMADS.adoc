== 1. What happens

Once per **single-human** game, shortly after the first city stands (round
≥ 4, or round ≥ 12 if no city yet), a neutral horde camps 9–13 tiles from
the player's court: two warriors and a scout, owned by `NOMAD_CIV_ID` (-2),
fortified, **announced** to the player (`NOMAD_SIGHTED`) — and NOT revealed:
the tents stand 9–13 tiles out, beyond your sight, so lighting them up would
paint ~50 never-walked tiles as explored (gray fog where you have never
been). Fog is earned by walking it; you meet them the honest way, when their
riders stop beside your city. They do not
stay with you: when the visit is over they strike camp and **ride on to the
next court** — every alive court in turn, though a *random* leg skips your
gate (only a promised return brings them back; see *The route* below).

One faction is rolled per game — the SAME people return on a return visit
(they are who you met). The three are drawn from real nomadic history, not
from game archetypes (research notes and art prompts:
`src/game/engine/nomad/FACTIONS_README.md`):

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Faction |Temperament

|**The Bedouin** (trader, 🐫)
|Arabia's camel caravans: the well, the duty of hospitality, the incense
roads. Bargains: knowledge ×0.8, normal hire, settles beside you often
(55%), tolerates three refusals.

|**The Mongol Horde** (warrior, 🐎)
|The steppe confederation: horse archers, the *yam* relay, the ger that
moves with the season. Proud: hire ×1.4, settles rarely (15%), one refusal
is tolerated — the second ends the meeting as hostility.

|**The Sámi** (mystic, 🦌)
|Sápmi's tundra: the reindeer migration, a sky read like a road.
Enigmatic: knowledge ×0.9, settles sometimes (30%), two refusals.
|===

The visit is **contact-based**: the camp never sends an envoy on a timer.
Its units **wander the map toward your court** (nearest city, else nearest
unit) — the walk zig-zags: most steps close in, some drift sideways, and
the distance to the court never grows. They walk it **as a pack**: one
leader, two riders inside `NOMAD_GROUP_RADIUS` of it, all of them limited
to the pace of the slowest (see *The roaming horde* below). The message
binds to the nomads
**reaching your gate**: the parley opens only when a nomad unit
stands beside your city (or beside one of your units) — no nomad at the
gate, no envoy, and the riders never enter your city. On contact the
audience screen opens with the faction's big art
(see `src/game/engine/nomad/FACTIONS_README.md`), name, epithet and a lead
line in the
clan's own voice, offering:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Door |Effect

|Buy their knowledge
|One technology the court lacks, for **half its raw research cost**
(clamped 25–100, × the clan's factor, × the **refusal penalty**: +25 %
per closed hand, four of them double it). A broke court receives it free
("the road gives"). The price and the technology are whatever the BUTTON
promised — never re-rolled while the modal is open. An **angry** tribe
tables no knowledge and no maps at all.

|Accept their maps *(first visit only)*
|`revealArea` — a radius-10 swath around the camp is revealed to the
player. No other game source reveals fog without walking it. (Not while
angry: no trade with a tribe you turned hostile.)

|Let them settle *(random offer, per clan chance)*
|Only after you explicitly agree, a settlement is planted on a tile
directly beside your city: the settlement TILE drains
**2 food/turn** from the city's balance (`cityFoodBalance`), raises
**defenders** (one warrior per settlement, yours), who stand on the city
perimeter and intercept attackers when the shared combat-odds calculation
approves the fight — **and buys that city
one extra trade-route slot** (`maxTradeRoutesFor`: 3 → 4, for as long as
the settlement stands). The camp **keeps producing**: one wanderer of its
own every 12 rounds (at most 2 alive), those patrol open tiles on the city's
perimeter, and the camp's day market pays your treasury **2 gold per
turn** for the food it eats (`NOMAD_SETTLEMENT_TRADE_GOLD`). The wanderers
are home — the standing camp is gone.

|Refuse the settlement *(same table)*
|No camp is planted. They asked to stay and you said no: **a closed hand**. `refusals += 1`,
their prices rise by `NOMAD_REFUSAL_PRICE_PENALTY` (25 %) for every door
afterwards, and past the clan's tolerance the refusal turns them hostile.
(The door only appears when the settlement was rolled — refusing is a
choice, not taking the other doors by default.)

|Hire the riders you met
|**Only the camp units that stand with you right now** — beside your city,
or within one tile of one of your units — at build cost × **5** ×
the faction's factor × the refusal penalty (a lone rider you met: 50 gold
at peace; the spawned three, if they all came to the gate: 175 — 245 for
the Mongols). Those units convert
to your colors, un-fortified and usable at once; **the rest of the tribe
keeps its tents**, its chest and its neutrality: they linger, an AI court
may still race for them, and the rest can be hired at a later meeting.
With nobody at the gate the door is not tabled at all, and answering it
from afar returns `notMeeting` — walking away is not an answer.

|Pass in peace
|They leave after 6 rounds instead of 20. Within their patience
(≥ refusals left) they simply part; beyond it — they turn.
|===

Whichever deal was struck (or none), the **camp remains a race**: from 16
rounds after the parley — late in the linger, a last chance as the tents are
about to strike — an eligible AI court may buy **ONE GROUP** off the tribe
(chance scales with the leader's diplomacy and is deliberately slim,
`NOMAD_AI_HIRE_BASE_CHANCE` 0.02 + 0.005 × diplomacy ≈ 18 % per visit).
Two rules bound it: a court **never buys the tribe's last camp** (the tribe
stays in the world), and the player is **told with a modal**, not a toast —
`NOMAD_GROUP_HIRED` opens the audience as a greeting, because half your
problem just changed uniform. If nobody buys, the group walks off after 20
rounds (`NOMAD_DEPARTED`).

A **second parley** fires when the camp stands with you after the first
answer — hire or farewell only. THE RULE: **a camp may never sit at your
gate without a dialog** — the tents move to the tile the envoy stood on
(your city pays their food for as long as they linger) and a dismissed
audience comes round again on its 3-round cooldown whenever they stand
with you or you walk up to their tents. The city screen carries the same
doors, plus a *Speak with them* button for the moments none is tabled.

=== The roaming horde — wander, growth and the split

The camp is a LIVING group on the map, not a scenery piece:

* **Three at the beginning, as one pack.** The horde starts as the spawned
  three (two warriors and a scout) and wanders in a group across the map on
  its way to your court — zig-zagging, never receding from the target, so
  the contact-based visit always lands. It travels TOGETHER: the rider
  nearest the court leads and the other two hold a `NOMAD_GROUP_RADIUS` (1)
  ring around it, and nobody may take more steps in a round than the
  slowest rider of the group — the scout walks 2 while the spears walk 1,
  so without that shared pace it covered twice the ground and arrived alone
  with its mates a long way behind. A leader blocked by a wall of swamp
  releases the ring for a round, so the pack can slow down but never cages
  itself.
* **Later with more.** Once the parley has been **answered**, the group
  gathers its own riders — one every `NOMAD_GROWTH_ROUNDS` (8), paid from
  the chest like everything else. (Before the answer the meeting stays the
  classic three-spear parley; only the steel stance may raise its small
  v1 garrison.)
* **At six, the great split.** When a group reaches
  `NOMAD_GROUP_SPLIT_SIZE` (6) living wanderers it DIVIDES: half keeps
  the parent camp, half walks to a nearby tile (`NOMAD_SPLIT_DISTANCE`,
  4) and plants the splinter's tents — announced as `NOMAD_SPLIT`. Both
  camps are ONE tribe: one chest, one strategy, two cooperating groups —
  and the tribe never keeps more than `NOMAD_MAX_CAMPS` (2) tents. Both
  groups regrow to full strength instead of splitting a third time, and
  no group ever masses past the split size.
* **Settled camps keep producing** (see the settle door): the mustered
  wanderers roam the map around the stakes on a `NOMAD_ROAM_LEASH` (6) —
  the tribe's living texture wherever it put down roots.

=== The route — from court to court

The tribe does not live at your gate: it travels. When a visit ends — a
deal, a refusal, the linger, or the 60-round contact window — the camp
strikes tents and **rides to the next court**, in a shuffled cycle of every
ALIVE court: **you first** (the story opens at your gate), then the
rivals, then round again. A *random* leg, though, never lands on YOUR
gate — the human seat is dropped from the route — so a refusal is not
undone a few rounds later. You are rejoined only by a promised return, or
when every rival is dead and you are the last court standing. A friendly
court keeps its promised return (+30 rounds) and goes to the FRONT of the
route; an angry court is torn off it (they owe you nothing).

The ride is a RIDE: the riders stay on the map and walk it step by step
(`marchRoad`), never a travel spell — you watch them cross the country
before they reach any gate. `NOMAD_TRAVEL_ROUNDS` (12, or +30 for the
promised return) is no longer an arrival time but the EARLIEST they may
raise tents at the next court: a group that arrives early holds a couple
of tiles out and mills there (never frozen, never standing ON a court
with no tents), and walks in when its hour comes. A group that cannot
make progress for `NOMAD_TRAVEL_ROUNDS` re-routes to another court — a
promised return never re-routes away — and a group wiped on the road
re-forms at its destination instead, so the tribe never vanishes.

At a rival there is no audience — the envoy is answered **in code**:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Answer |When

|Buys the knowledge |the rival has diplomacy ≥ 3, the gold, and a technology still to lack → `NOMAD_KNOWLEDGE`: their chest pays, the tech lands in their tree

|Hires ONE GROUP at their gate |(a gamble) there is a second camp to sell, they can afford it and they roll high → `NOMAD_GROUP_HIRED` — a modal for you, never the last camp

|Camps and moves on |otherwise — the same linger as at your gate, then the route continues
|===

The player hears the DEALS as news, never as a modal: `NOMAD_PARLEY` (and
the audience screen) is yours alone, and `visits` counts only YOUR court —
the one exception is a group sale (`NOMAD_GROUP_HIRED`), which is worth
your screen. A camp that never finds the door (an unreachable island, a
city with no room) rides on after `NOMAD_CONTACT_TIMEOUT_ROUNDS` (60).

=== One group travels, one stays

A grown tribe keeps **two camps** (`NOMAD_GROUP_SPLIT_SIZE` = 6 riders →
the group divides, `NOMAD_MAX_CAMPS` = 2), and the two are **independent
travellers under the one brain**: each carries its own court, its own
linger and its own answer (`NomadCamp`). When one group strikes tents:

* its RIDERS ride on across the map and raise new tents around
  themselves at the next court — no earlier than `NOMAD_TRAVEL_ROUNDS`
  later (or at the promised return, +30 rounds), and never by a spell;
* **the other group keeps standing where it is** — its own linger runs
  from that round, so it leaves a travel spell later;
* and because somebody is always left standing, **the tribe never vanishes
  from the map** the way a single camp used to wander into the fog.

=== On the map — seen coming, pitched, and riding the long road

* **They are always on show.** Nomad units (`NOMAD_CIV_ID`) are drawn
  straight through the fog — riders and travelling bazaars alike — so you
  watch the tribe cross the map long before it reaches your gate, and
  watch it ride on afterwards. Only the TRAVELLERS show: the terrain
  underneath stays unexplored and stays black, so looking at them lifts no
  fog (the grey-on-unvisited-tiles bug this replaced).
* **A standing camp pitches a tent** on the first free tile beside its
  tents (`tile.nomadTent`, drawn with the clan's icon over ⛺): beside YOUR city when the group
  camps at your gate — the visible "somebody lives here" that says why
  their riders stand still — and out in the country at the spawn. The
  pitch is struck with the tents; a settlement keeps one forever (it is
  drawn from `tile.nomadSettlement`, which also carries the food drain).
* **A bazaar refuses the short road.** `NOMAD_CARAVAN_MIN_JOURNEY` (8):
  born beside its target it would deliver in the very round it was built
  and never be seen crossing the map, so `pickRouteTarget` prefers the
  nearest partner AT JOURNEY DISTANCE and only takes the short road when
  nothing is farther away.

=== The city screen knows them

Open the city the camp stands at (or the one their settlement feeds) and
the overview shows a **Nomads at the gate** section: their poster, name,
epithet and standing; what the camp costs you (`NOMAD_CAMP_FOOD_COST` per
rider per turn) and what the settlement costs and buys (food, defender,
the extra trade route); and the **doors they have on the table right now**
— answered straight from the city screen, same engine, same promised
terms. With nothing tabled there is a *Speak with them* button
(`NomadManager.speakAtGate`), so a camp at your gate is never silent.

=== Equivalent units — their chest, not their shields

The tribe never builds: it **buys**, and what it buys mirrors what the
players can field. As soon as ANY player has a ground soldier available,
the equivalent exists for the tribe — **same numbers, only a different name
and a different picture** (`UnitConstants.NOMAD_MIRRORED_UNITS`):

* `nomad_foot_<base>` — a **1:1 copy** ("Nomad Riflemen"), the picture of
  the soldier it mirrors;
* `nomad_camel_<base>` — the same soldier **mounted**: +1 movement,
  ×1.5 gold ("Mounted Riflemen"), foot soldiers only — nobody puts a tank
  on a camel.

What the camp recruits is the **strongest soldier any player can build
right now** (`bestMirroredBase`): archers while nobody has researched
anything, armour the moment somebody can field it. Alternate musters take
the mounted shape, so a grown camp carries both kinds. Every one of them
carries `requires: 'nomad_bazaar_only'`, so **no production list — player,
AI or auto — ever offers one**: the chest is their only currency
(production points are the players').

Each faction wears its own beast on the mounted shape
(`NOMAD_MOUNT_ICONS`): a Bedouin **camel**, a Mongol **horse**, a Sámi
**reindeer** — same stats, different pictures.

=== What a camp standing at your gate costs

* **A visiting camp** costs its host city `NOMAD_CAMP_FOOD_COST` (1) of
  food **per rider per turn** while any of them stands inside the
  settlement drain radius (3) of the city — they eat your grain whether or
  not you invited them, and the bill stops the moment they ride off
  (`EconomicManager.nomadDrain`, read by the projection, the growth
  pipeline and the AI governor alike).
* **A settlement** (the settle door) costs `NOMAD_SETTLEMENT_FOOD_COST`
  (2) per turn for as long as it stands — and buys that city **one extra
  trade-route slot** (`maxTradeRoutesFor`: `MAX_TRADE_ROUTES` 3 → 4), on
  top of its defenders and its day market.
* **They come with caravans**: the moment the envoy first speaks at a
  court, one of the tribe's travelling bazaars is with the camp (paid from
  the chest like every other wagon, once per visit), so a visit that opens
  no door still leaves a merchant on the road.

== 2. Their own books — an AI player's economy

The nomads are a player, not scenery: one treasury funds every door of the
feature, which is where the balance lives. All of it is session state in
`src/game/engine/nomad/` — `NomadManager` (the state machine) takes its
orders from `NomadAI` (the strategy brain, below). There is no
`civilizations[-2]` entry, so a city lookup for the nomads misses exactly
the way a barbarian city's indexed lookup does, and rendering falls back to
the same neutral colour.

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Piece |Rule

|War chest
|Opens with **300 gold** (`NOMAD_START_GOLD`). Bazaars, bribes, musters and
city purchases all draw from this one purse — spend on soldiers and the
city market waits, spend on a city and the bribes do.

|Travelling Bazaar *(new unit type)*
|`travelling_bazaar` (`UNIT_TYPES.NOMAD_CARAVAN`) — movement 2, defense 1,
**cost 100** — a third of the opening purse, on purpose: a route is an
INVESTMENT the economy has to pay back. Built ONLY by the camp (gated by an
impossible tech name in `UNIT_PROPERTIES.requires` and
`UNIT_TECH_REQUIREMENTS`, so no player or AI production list ever offers
one), every **8 rounds**, at most **2 in flight**. It walks its full
movement toward its partner each round along a real A* path
(`findPath`, routing around parked units — a greedy walk gets lost around
a bay) and is
**consumed on delivery** (Civ1 rule). A bazaar blocked for 10 rounds or on
the road for 40 is lost — no route, no refund.

|Trade routes
|On delivery: **3 + floor(pop/2) gold per turn** per partner, up to
**six routes** (`routes + in-flight ≥ 6` refuses the build — a seventh
city opens no seventh route). The route dies with its partner city, or the
moment that city changes hands: conquer a nomad trade partner and you cut
their income.

|Bought cities
|A city the nomads bought pays **1 gold per head per turn** until it is
retaken or razed (`NOMAD_CITY_TRIBUTE_PER_POP`).

|The city market — *peace*
|With **450+ gold** in the chest, an answered parley and no anger, the
tribe table an offer for the player's biggest city: **250 + 75 × pop**,
one offer per 15 rounds, refused cities are never offered again — and the
player's LAST city is never offered at all (selling it would end the story
via `applyLastCityRule`; a market that could take your final city would be
a trap, not a deal). The sale is peaceful: gold changes hands, the Palace
cannot travel (stripped, the seller re-seats its capital), production stops
(the nomads have no governor) and the city begins paying tribute. The
audience carries its own eyebrow — *The Nomad Market* — so the e2e dialog
sweep can tell it from the parley.

|Bribes — *anger*
|While ANGRY, the same purse buys treachery: every 3 rounds the horde pays
`bribeUnitCost` for the nearest player unit within 4 tiles — the unit
switches to `-2`, joins the raiding party, and the chest bleeds. Own
conversion, because `DiplomacyManager.bribeUnit` debits
`civilizations[civId].gold`, which does not exist for the nomads.

|Camp muster & growth
|The camp fashions its own soldiers — paid for like everything else.
**Before the answer** only the steel stance musters, at most **2 alive
beyond the spawned horde** (`NOMAD_CAMP_MUSTER_LIMIT`, one every 8
rounds) — the meeting stays the classic three. **After the answer** the
group simply GROWS: one rider every `NOMAD_GROWTH_ROUNDS` (8),
stance-blind, until the group is full and divides (see *The roaming
horde*). WHAT they buy mirrors the players: the strongest ground soldier
any player can build, as their own equivalent (see *Equivalent units*) —
a foot copy, or on alternate musters the same soldier mounted. The nomads
are TYPICALLY money makers: soldiers are the exception, not the norm, and
every one of them is **bought from the chest — production points are never
involved**.

|Settled camp production
|A settlement keeps producing: one wanderer of its own every
`NOMAD_SETTLEMENT_MUSTER_ROUNDS` (12), at most
`NOMAD_SETTLEMENT_MUSTER_LIMIT` (2) alive — those patrol open tiles on the
host city's perimeter (never entering the city) and are
NEVER part of the camp machinery (no parley, no anger, no departing).
Its day market pays the host **`NOMAD_SETTLEMENT_TRADE_GOLD` (2) gold
per turn** — the trade half of the deal, against the tile's food drain
and beside its defenders.

|Knowledge brokerage
|Every 15 rounds the tribe sells a technology to an AI court — half the
raw research cost, clan-adjusted, clamped 25–100 (`NOMAD_KNOWLEDGE_*`) →
gold in the chest, knowledge across the world. (The player buys the same
wares at the parley; this is the other counter of their shop.)

|The plea — *ask for money*
|Broke (under 80 gold) and peaceful, the tribe tables a plea for **60
gold**: give it and they remember who filled their chest (standing turns
`friendly`); refuse and it counts as a refusal — past the clan's tolerance
the plea becomes a grudge. One plea per 25 rounds, its own eyebrow (*The
Nomads Ask*) so the e2e sweep can dismiss it.

|Barbarian friendship
|Nomads and barbarians never fight each other: `BarbarianManager.act()`
skips every negative-id unit and city (the nomads are friends of the
horde), and when the barbarians TAKE a city the tribe strikes camp and
**moves in beside it** (`onBarbariansTookCity` → `NOMAD_MIGRATED`, told
as a full-screen chronicle card) and a
friendship route opens (it dies with the city, as any route does). An
angry tribe keeps its revenge and stays put.

|The balance
|Anger and the city market are mutually exclusive (`standing` gates
both), routes fund whichever door is open, and every door spends the same
coins the bazaars earn: 300 start → a bazaar costs 100 and takes 8 rounds
→ routes trickle in → 450 opens the market, or a war opens the purse.
|===

=== The strategy brain (NomadAI)

Every **8 rounds** — and at once the moment anger takes it — the tribe
re-scores the world and may change doctrine. Each stance is a score over
routes still open, chest fullness, war-readiness, the clan's temperament
and WHERE IN HISTORY they are, plus a small random jitter (two identical
games drift apart). The winner becomes the round's plan, and a change is
**announced** (`NOMAD_STRATEGY` → toast) — strategy you can watch.

[cols="1,2,3", options="header"]
|===
|Stance |Typical round |What the plan does

|`roads`
|early — and any time cities wait unsigned
|build travelling bazaars, broker knowledge — the money maker

|`treasury`
|mid, when the vault is filling
|build nothing, keep filling the chest (and beg if it runs dry)

|`market`
|450+ in the chest, world ≥ 40 rounds
|table city offers — this stance also WAIVES the 450 gate (the price gate
still holds)

|`steel`
|warrior clans, world ≥ 60, camp has room
|muster the camp (up to the limit) while still running the roads

|`bribery`
|angry — forced, not scored
|no trade, no cities, no musters: every coin goes to treachery
|===

== 3. The relationship (memory)

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|Standing |How it is earned |What it buys

|`neutral`
|The default; a tolerated refusal.
|Nothing — the camp may still be raced for.

|`friendly`
|Taking ANY deal (knowledge, maps, hire).
|**One return visit** 30 rounds after they leave: the same clan comes
back (`returnLead` line) and, as their gift, charts a **radius-14**
stretch of the unknown world automatically (`NOMAD_RETURN_GIFT`) — the
paid doors remain, the maps door does not (the chart WAS the gift).

|`angry`
|**First blood** (any camp unit injured or missing — combat's signature),
or refusals beyond the clan's tolerance (Mongol: one, Sámi: two,
Bedouin: three).
|**Raids** for 12 rounds: the camp wakes, attacks anything adjacent
(`moveUnit`, `canUnitMoveTo`-gated so a wide river cannot stall the
march), and at the gates steals **15% of the treasury (5–30 gold)** every
3 rounds (`NOMAD_ANGRY`, `NOMAD_RAID`). Then the camp is gone — and anger
never earns a return visit.
|===

== 4. Rules and boundaries

* **One human seat (or none), every court visited.** The feature runs in a
  game with exactly one human — hotseat is still out (it would have to pick
  whose screen opens) — and in an **all-AI spectator game**, where there is
  nobody to talk to: the tribe spawns once at an AI court and rides between
  them, but no sighting and no audience ever fires. Inside a single-human
  game the tribe travels **player to player**: your court first, then every
  AI rival, in a shuffled cycle. Your court gets the audience; the rivals
  get the deal struck in code. A later leg only leads back to your gate on
  a return the tribe *promised* to a friendly court (§3) — a random ride
  skips you after a refusal, unless every rival is dead and you are the only
  court left.
* **Their own player, neutral by default — and nobody swings first.** Civ id
  `-2` — deliberately NOT the barbarians' `-1`, which `BarbarianManager`
  drives (it would commandeer the camp, and would put bought cities under
  barbarian AI control). Contact recording skips negative ids and no civ is
  at war with the camp. `AIUtility.isHostileTo` is the ONE predicate behind
  every AI attack, march and threat reading; for the tribe it answers *yes*
  only when the camp has turned **angry** or that civ's **committed war plan
  names it** — the same pipeline that starts every other war this civ
  fights. Until then the ruler AI walks around the camp instead of into it:
  a GoTo route that reaches it is dropped rather than stepped into
  (`TurnManager.neutralNomadInWay`), the AI's own step refuses the tile
  (`AIManager.moveAsAI`), and a camp beside a city is not counted as a
  siege (`collectCityThreatSamples` — it would otherwise pull the garrison
  out and cancel offensives for a camp that cannot strike). So the two
  sides use each other: the courts hire the riders, buy the knowledge the
  tribe brokers, and leave the first blood where it belongs.
* **Session-only.** Nothing here is persisted; a loaded game simply has no
  nomads (v1). `NomadManager.reset()` puts the rest of them back in the
  box with the state: the PITCH (`tile.nomadTent`) and every rider or
  bazaar the save carried are dropped, because with no camp, no road and
  no settlement spot behind them nothing would ever move them again — and
  nomad units are drawn through the fog, so an orphan would stand there
  in plain sight forever. The settlement's DEFENDERS are your own units
  and stay where you paid for them. The settlement TILE flag persists —
  its food/defense effects are engine code that reads the map, so they
  survive saves.
* **Dismissal is not refusal — and silence is not an option.** Closing
  the audience without answering leaves the parley open: it re-offers on
  its 3-round cooldown while the camp **stands with you** (beside your city or
  beside a unit) or you stand within 2 of its tents, never every round and
  never not at all. A dismissed city offer (or plea) re-tables the same
  way, and dies with the camp (or its city) so the serialization slot can
  never strand.
* **The hall reads, the gate acts.** Once sighted the tribe is listed in
  the diplomacy window with its own row and panel (`hallState`), but every
  door except *locating their camp* stays locked until your units stand
  with theirs — `resolveParley('hire')` re-checks the meeting and answers
  `notMeeting` rather than selling riders you never met.
* **Friends of the horde.** Nomads and barbarians are allies by default:
  `BarbarianManager.act()` only ever attacks positive-id units and cities,
  and a barbarian conquest moves the nomad camp in beside it.
* Three audiences are **blocking dialogs** — `e2e/game.spec.ts`
  `BLOCKING_DIALOGS` dismisses the parley with *Pass in peace*, the market
  with *Refuse — the city stays yours* and the plea with *Refuse — no gold
  for wanderers* (the first refusal never angers any clan) so tests keep
  pace.
