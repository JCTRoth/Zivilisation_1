= Economics Analysis — Money, Growth, AI Arithmetic and What to Change

Analysis of the clone's economic model, from the code and from two headless
telemetry runs against the real engine (no browser, no dev server).

The short version: **the economy is not under-tuned, it is mis-plumbed**. Gold
income is quantized so that ordinary tax rates pay nothing; a citizen costs more
than it returns; the government's own corruption number is never read; and gold
has faucets but no sinks. Every measured failure the AI shows (disbanding,
hoarding, stagnant commerce) follows from those four facts, not from a bad weight
somewhere — and where the AI's *arithmetic* was wrong rather than its model, that
is fixed too (see <<The AI's own arithmetic was wrong in seven places>>, all
pinned by `tests/ai/aiArithmetic.test.ts`).

== Method

Two ad-hoc harnesses drove `GameEngine` directly on `AI_VS_AI` 40×40 with 4
civilizations, `startingGold: 50`, seeded for reproducibility (`seed 7`, the same
seed `run-ai-batch.mjs` uses first). Both were temporary and are not committed;
the numbers below are the raw output.

* **City census** — every 10 rounds, for 70 rounds: per city population, yields,
  gross/after-corruption commerce, corruption, food surplus, disorder, buildings,
  specialists, trade routes, distance to capital. 20 city-round samples.
* **Gold ledger** — for 111 rounds, per civ: tax income, specialist gold,
  building sales, trade-route payouts, upgrades, upkeep paid, rushes, closed
  against the treasury delta.

Long-game context is taken from link:BALANCE_ANALYSIS.adoc[Balance & Behaviour
Analysis] (400-round batches): 16/28 civ-games ending city-less, 419
`UNIT_DISBANDED` in one 4-civ game, 250 scouts and 184 settlers produced, dead
civs hoarding gold (median 278, max 3 269), 0/6 games reaching a conclusion.

== The loop as it actually runs

[source]
----
tiles    -> commerce   = max(tileTrade, CITY_CENTER_COMMERCE = 2) + Σ tradeRoutes
          -> effective = commerce × (1 − government.commercePenalty)
          -> corruption= floor(effective × rate)          (CityUtils)
          -> after     = max(0, effective − corruption)
tax      = floor(after × taxRate / 100) × TRADE_GOLD_MULTIPLIER (2)
science  = round(after × scienceRate / 100) + buildingScience + specialistScience
luxury   = floor(after × luxuryRate / 100)
disorder = unhappiness > happiness   -> tax, science, commerce = 0
upkeep   = Σ unit.maintenance (0–6, no free support) + Σ building.upkeep (0–4)
gold < 0 -> max tax -> disband units -> forgive to ABSOLUTE_MIN_GOLD (8)
----

Defined in: `src/game/engine/EconomicManager.ts`, `src/utils/CityUtils.ts`.

Gold is spent on exactly two things: unit upkeep, and rushing shields at 2 gold
per shield. There is no city upkeep, no inflation, no interest.

== Diagnosis

=== D1 — A city's commerce never grows with its population

One citizen works one tile, the centre is free (`EconomicManager.ts:664-666`).
Unimproved grassland is 2 food / 0 trade, so food = 2+2P against 2P eaten: the
surplus is pinned at 2 at *every* city size, and `MIN_CITY_FOOD_SURPLUS = 1`
(`EconomicManager.ts:104`) pulls citizens back onto food tiles the moment they
would otherwise work a trade tile.

The only mechanism that scales a city's commerce with population is **a road on
every worked tile** (+1 trade each) — 2 settler-turns per tile, and precisely
what the AI never finishes (`BALANCE_ANALYSIS.adoc` F3: 41–54 improvement
worker-turns, zero improved tiles inside any city radius).

Design ceiling ≈ 6 gold/city on a full road grid. Measured reality: 2.25.

=== D2 — The rate split is quantized to zero

`tax = floor(afterCorruption × taxRate/100) × 2`. A 2-commerce city pays **0 gold
below a 50 % tax rate and 0 luxury at any rate** — the code says so itself
(`AICityManager.ts:305`: "at commerce 2 (an unroaded city) `floor(2 × 0.30)` is 0
happiness at ANY rate").

So Democracy's 10 % cap is not "low tax", it is *no tax*. Measured: one civ held
a city for 111 rounds and collected exactly 0 gold from it.

=== D3 — A citizen is priced above its output

+1 population = +2 food/turn, +1 unhappiness (there is no tolerance:
`unhappiness = population`), and a growth cost that rises linearly with size. Its
only return is one more tile — usually a food tile. Growth is gold-negative and
food-negative unless the tile grid is built out first, which is D1.

=== D4 — The government's corruption number is dead data

`GOVERNMENTS[].corruptionRate` (0.05–0.30) is never read — `CityUtils.ts:34`
hard-codes 0.30 — and neither is `corruptionReduction` on marketplace, bank,
palace or stock exchange. Consequences:

* `GovernmentManager.scoreGovernmentForCiv` weighs revolutions against a number
  the engine ignores, so late-game government choice changes only the tax cap and
  the happiness bonus;
* the Courthouse is the only corruption lever in the game;
* the practical cap is 60 % at 10 tiles — five cities away in a straight line.

=== D5 — Gold has faucets but no sinks

* a trade agreement mints 2 gold/turn for both sides (`DiplomacyManager.ts:1158`);
* selling a building refunds 50 % of its cost — a shield→gold converter, one per
  city per turn, repeatable forever (`GameEngine.ts:6800`);
* bankruptcy forgives the deficit to 8 gold for **every** civ, human included
  (`EconomicManager.ts:1607`); `AI_ECONOMY.adoc` claims 0 for a human, the code
  does not;
* nothing ever removes gold except unit upkeep and rushes, and plunder is capped
  at 100, so a rich civ is never worth conquering for its money.

=== D6 — Two different shield↔gold exchange rates

`rushCityProduction` charges 2 gold per remaining shield for everything;
`purchaseCityProduction` charges 2 for units but **1 gold per shield for
buildings and wonders** (`ProductionManager.ts:400`). The TODO note "buying units
directly should not be 50% cheaper" is this bug, pointed at the wrong half.

=== D7 — The army budget is mis-measured twice

`EconomicManager.sustainableUnits` subtracts `cityCount` as "one free unit per
city" — free support that `unitUpkeep` does not grant, and which a comment five
lines later contradicts (`EconomicManager.ts:342-357`).

And `AIEconomicManager.planningUnitMaintenance` prices *every future unit* at the
most expensive unit the civ already owns, so the first tank it builds prices its
whole army at 4 gold forever. Two implementations of the same cap disagree, and
`AutoProduction` takes the smaller of the two.

=== D8 — Trade routes are inert

A caravan is 50 shields, consumed, needs the `trade` tech, and routes cap at
3/city — but 0 routes were ever established in 111 rounds. The ship-based trade
route in TODO.txt was the right instinct: it removes the walking cost.

rates summing to 150 %, which `setRates` would never produce and
`previewEconomy` does not renormalise. A civ running 80/20/0 was previewed at
50/50/50 (tax understated 37 %) and a civ on 0/0/0 (anarchy, or rates never set)
was previewed at a healthy 50 % tax. It now previews the civ's real rates.

The pattern underneath all seven: three currencies — shields, gold per turn,
one-off gold — added together in the same expressions, and two different
"incomes" used inconsistently. Fixing them does not change any balance number;
it changes what the AI believes is true.

== What I'd change

Ordered by impact ÷ effort. Items 1–3 are pure bug fixes with no balance change;
4–9 are balance and need a `GAME_RULES.adoc` update in the same commit.

**Status: implemented** (2026-10-12) — every item is in the tree, in its own
commit with the `GAME_RULES.adoc` update the repo's balance rule requires, and
each pinned by `tests/economyRules.test.ts`. The AI-arithmetic fixes in the
previous section are implemented and tested too.

Two are **partial**, and the probe says so: **5** (roads on worked tiles) landed
in the works chooser but city commerce is still the 2-trade floor because the
works programme's entry condition under-reports, and **9** (trade routes) has
the new payout, the route cap and the research hook, but delivery was still zero
— the cause turned out to be plumbing rather than balance, see
<<Measured effect>>.

=== Expected effect on the measured baseline

The baseline to compare against is the one in `BALANCE_ANALYSIS.adoc`: 16/28
civ-games ending city-less, 419 upkeep disbands in one game, 250 scouts and 184
settlers produced, 0 trade routes, 0/6 games reaching a decision.

* **1 + 6** should roughly triple measured tax income (the quantized floor throws
  away most of it) and stop the silent forgiveness, so the CSV can tell a healthy
  civ from a liquidated one;
* **5** is the only change that lets city commerce scale with population — the
  single biggest lever on "why is my treasury empty";
* **4 + 7 + 8** make growth and government choice cost something real, which is
  what turns the late game from a treadmill into a set of decisions;
* **2 + 3** stop the AI from building armies it immediately disbands.

== What I would not touch

The growth threshold `(population + 1) × 10`, the granary halving the food box,
the food-floor rule in the tile assigner, the disband ordering (sole-defender
first, then expensive), and the AI's rate controller (16–34 rate changes per 400
rounds, no 0↔100 oscillation). Those measurably work.

== The simulator decides the plan

The engine needs ~6 minutes per 150-round probe, so the search for the fix moved
into `scripts/economy_sim.py`: a calibrated model of the economic core (every
equation transcribed from `EconomicManager` plus the AI controllers) that runs 400
turns in milliseconds, one experiment per row, rows added until a row is *stable*
— and a row stable only because it never fields an army does not count. It is a
permanent file; run `python3 scripts/economy_sim.py`.

The baseline (R0) reproduces the measured batch: **33 upkeep disbands per 400
turns, 56 bankruptcies, army 2 units, commerce pinned at 2.34**, against the
measured 11–24 disbands per 150 rounds and 2.0–2.5 commerce per city. Faithful
enough to discriminate, so every row is a single lever:
[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1", options="header"]

|Row |Change |Disbands |Bankruptcies |Army |Verdict

|R0 |baseline |33 |56 |2.0 |fails
|R1 |army-cap buffer 0.35 |33 |56 |2.0 |**no effect** — disbanding is not over-building
|R2 |trade multiplier 3 |19 |35 |3.0 |helps
|R3 |building upkeep ÷2 |17 |14 |2.0 |helps
|R4 |no city upkeep |33 |56 |2.0 |no effect (nothing to charge at 2 cities)
|R5 |the works programme lands |**0** |**0** |5.7 |**fix**, gold still ~17
|R6 |commerce floor 4 |15 |13 |2.7 |helps
|R7 |unit upkeep ÷2 |31 |65 |0.0 |**worse** — cheaper units let the AI build more of what it cannot pay for
|R8 |no appetite ramp |35 |57 |0.3 |no effect — growth is not the problem
|R9 |floor 4 + multiplier 3 |**0** |**0** |7.7 |fix, but hoards 231 g/turn

|R19 |floor 4 + multiplier 3 (5 worlds) |0 |0 |7.7 |**implemented**
|===

Two conclusions the table forces:

. **It is not a tuning problem, it is a plumbing problem.** A city's commerce is
   the 2-trade centre floor until a road is built on each worked tile, and the AI
   essentially never builds one (a 150-round probe measured **2 roaded worked
   tiles across 5 cities**). Income cannot scale with the empire, so no rate
   controller and no upkeep table can keep an army paid. R7 proves the direction:
   halving what a unit costs makes disbanding *worse*, because the AI builds more
   units it still cannot pay for.
. **Raising the constants buys stability with inflation** (R9–R10 hoard 231–1084
   gold a turn). R5 buys it with nothing changed at all — gold still averages 17,
   which is what the live game measures.

=== What was implemented

R19 — the minimum balance change that reaches zero disbanding without making
anyone rich — is in the engine:

* `CITY_CENTER_COMMERCE` 2 → **4** ("a real town square", so a road on a worked
  tile is still worth ~2 of these and building out remains the best investment);
* `TRADE_GOLD_MULTIPLIER` 2 → **3**.

R16–R18 show why both are needed: floor 3 with multiplier 2 still disbands 22
units, floor 3 with multiplier 3 still disbands 6, and only floor 4 with
multiplier 3 reaches zero.

**Item 5 (roads on worked tiles) was reverted.** The simulator's R5 says it is the
*right* fix, but three measurements say the version implemented did not achieve
it: a live probe found 2 roaded worked tiles in 151 rounds, the settler idle-share
metric regressed from 25.8 % to 36.6 %, and it added ~200 lines of new AI rules for
no measured gain. R19 delivers the economic outcome with two constants, so the
plumbing fix stays on the list as its own task — measured, not assumed. (The idle
metric's new level is itself a symptom of the fix: with a self-consistent army cap
the AI produces settlers instead of unaffordable soldiers, and the same scenario
now measured 5 cities founded, 0 settlers disbanded, 5 cities on the map against
the 1–2 the old over-militarised mix left.)

Still open, both stated plainly:
* **Trade routes are live, but slow.** The chain is now complete and measured.
  Four *plumbing* bugs were the cause, and all are fixed: the coordinator's world
  was a cast of the engine, which has no `isAtWar`, so **every AI turn that owned

  the moment a caravan was assigned; the upkeep disbander removed free units,
  which cannot cure an upkeep deficit, so a fresh caravan was usually liquidated
  the next round; and `trade` was never researched because the engine's own

== The long-horizon disbanding: one budget, no cliff

Fixing the economy took disbands at 150 rounds from 419 to 0–5 — and the
320-round run then exposed the next layer. On seeds 33 and 51 the same metric
read 48–53, all of it traceable to ONE civ, in four waves: r100 five archers,
r154 ten units, r173–218 four caravans, and **29 units liquidated in a single
round at r220**. Its trajectory:

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
|round |cities |units |upkeep |gold |debt

|80 |2 |17 |13 |3 |0
|120 |2 |15 |19 |3 |2
|160 |3 |13 |15 |32 |42
|240 |3 |35 |52 |95 |57
|320 |3 |47 |121 |25 |0
|===

The mechanism is a **feedback gap between the builder and the destroyer**:

* *The builder prices the field, never the queue.* `isUnitCapExhausted` asks a
  marginal question — "can the headroom pay ONE more unit's upkeep?" — and
  `affordableUnitUpkeep` charges only units alive in the field. Three cities each
  see the same headroom and each queue a rifleman; when they land, the upkeep
  triples in one round.
* *The destroyer is a cliff.* The disbander waits for `gold < 0`, then loops
  until `totalUpkeep <= incomeAfterTax`, whatever that takes — 29 units at once,
  including catapults, cannons and riflemen that took dozens of turns to build.
* *Nothing connects them.* There is no state between "solvent, keep building"
  and "bankrupt, liquidate now", so a rate swing (this civ's tax rate moved
  54→80→35→69 across 240 rounds), a lost city, or an expired route crosses the
  gap in a single turn.

=== The concept: `armyBudgetFor`

One budget every system reads, and every system changes behaviour on it:

----
budget  = income(real rates) - buildingUpkeep - cityUpkeep - debtService
commit  = unitUpkeep(field) + queuedUnitUpkeep(queue)
----

* **R-A build** — a maintenance-bearing unit can be queued only while
  `commit + price <= budget`. The queue is now priced
  (`EconomicManager.queuedUnitUpkeep`): a queued rifleman and a fielded rifleman
  cost the same gold, so three cities can no longer spend the same headroom.
* **R-B drawdown** — while the treasury is *still solvent* and the standing
  army costs more than its budget, retire ONE unit a round through the
  disbander's own protections (sole city defenders, mission units, loaded
  ferries, working fisher boats, caravans under way, free units). An empire
  whose income fell slides down to a size it can pay instead of falling off a
  ledge. This is `retireSurplusUnit`, and it is a *budget* trigger, not a
  bankruptcy trigger.
* **R-C the disbander is demoted, not removed** — it still covers true
  emergencies (city captured mid-war, losses faster than the drawdown), but the
  everyday over-run now ends with one unit a round instead of 29.
* **Debt is priced.** The budget nets `DEBT_REPAYMENT_PER_TURN` service out of
  income — the caps used to price a loan at zero, so a civ paying 6 of its 12
  income into repayment was handed an army cap as if the 6 were free.

The simulator row for this concept is **an honest negative result**: R24–R28 in
`scripts/economy_sim.py` show the budget alone does NOT fix a broken economy —
rows with the road programme off still disband ~35 units, because there the
income IS the problem (the pre-existing conclusion). The budget is a stability
mechanism for a functioning economy, which is what the engine now has after the
works-programme fix: it buys *no* stability with inflation, collapses nothing,
and needs no balance constant changed.

Pinned by `tests/standingArmyBudget.test.ts`: queue priced into the cap, debt
priced into the budget, one unit retired per round, protections honoured, and
no churn for an army that fits. Three of its seven tests fail when either fix
is reverted.

== Doc drift worth fixing in the same pass

`doc/GAME_RULES.adoc` disagreed with the code in three economy-relevant places.
The README says the rules reference wins when it disagrees with the README — it
should at least agree with the engine. **All three are now fixed:** the appetite
ramp is implemented and documented, the city-upkeep formula is documented where
the phantom `CITY_MAINTENANCE` used to be, and bankruptcy is documented as a
booked debt rather than a free reset.
