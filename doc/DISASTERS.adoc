= City Disasters — The Seven Civ1 Events, Retuned

> *Module:* `src/game/engine/DisasterManager.ts`
> *Hook:* `src/game/engine/TurnManager.ts` — `processTurnEvents`, for every city, after growth and production
> *Event:* `CITY_DISASTER` → `src/utils/EngineEventHandlers.ts` (`onCityDisaster`)
> *Sound:* `cityDisaster` — see `doc/SOUND_EFFECTS.adoc`
> *Strings:* `event.disaster.*` in all six dictionaries
> *Tests:* `tests/disasters.test.ts` (rules, effects, frequency), `tests/sfxWiring.test.ts` (the cue's gate)

---

== Overview

Civ1 rolls a random disaster for a city during turn processing. With a
decent-sized empire the player met one most turns — usually a flood — which
turns a catastrophe into background noise and, worse, into a tax on the units
standing in the city. This implementation keeps the Civ1 rule set and changes
only the clock and the punch:

* **Far rarer.** The seven chances sum to well under 0.5% per eligible city
  per turn — roughly one disaster per 230 city-turns. A twenty-city empire
  sees one every dozen turns or so; *any specific* city seeing *any specific*
  disaster is a once-in-a-game event. The flood, the one that pestered the
  player, is deliberately the rarest of the seven.
* **A little harder.** Civ1's population losses (25–33%) are raised a notch
  (33–40%), and the secondary losses land in full: fire and earthquakes
  destroy a building, the volcano kills *and* burns, and pirates empty the
  food **and** shield stores while robbing the treasury.

[[table]]
== The Seven, with Their Rules

[cols="1,3,2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Disaster | Trigger | Prevention | What it does

| Famine
| Size 2+, Pottery known
| Granary
| Kills 40% of the population (at least one citizen, never the last one) and
  empties the food stores.

| Flood
| On or next to a river, Masonry known
| City Walls
| Drowns 33% of the population. The rarest roll in the game — see <<tuning>>.

| Fire
| Size 2+, Construction known, at least one destroyable building
| Aqueduct
| Burns one random non-Palace, non-Wonder building.

| Plague
| Size 2+, Construction known
| Aqueduct **or** Medicine
| Kills 33% of the population.

| Pirates
| Next to deep ocean, with gold or stores worth taking
| Barracks
| Steals max(10% of the treasury, 20 gold) and resets the food and shield
  stocks to 0.

| Volcano
| On or next to mountains, Ceremonial Burial known
| Temple
| Kills 40% of the population and burns one non-Palace, non-Wonder building
  (when there is one left to burn).

| Earthquake
| Next to hills, at least one destroyable building
| — (unpreventable)
| Destroys one random non-Palace, non-Wonder building. The only defence is
  geography.
|===

The Palace and World Wonders are never destroyed: a random event may humble an
empire's industry, never un-build its history or raze its capital building —
the same rule city capture follows. A disaster never removes the last citizen;
a city may be humbled, never erased. Famine *is* starvation's knife, but the
Granary's protection is checked per event, not per citizen.

[[cities-only]]
== Cities Only — Units Are Never Touched

A disaster targets the **city**. No event in the manager has a code path to a
unit: a flood never damages, drowns or destroys a military unit — ancient
levy or mechanised infantry — and neither does any of the other six. This is
an explicit design decision, not an omission:

* Civ1's disaster table is a city table; random unit attrition was never part
  of it.
* Units cannot dodge an event that fires during the turn flip, so "damaged by
  a flood" is pure lottery against an army the player placed.
* The modern-era complaint (an era where armies should manage a river) is
  already old by the time a legion is standing in a city that has seen fifty
  floods.

Randomness belongs in the city ledger, not in the order of battle. Every
disaster is announced as a city event, and `tests/disasters.test.ts` pins that
a unit standing in the struck city comes out with its health and existence
untouched.

[[ordering]]
== One per City, in a Fixed Order

Each eligible city rolls once per turn, cycling through the disaster table in
a fixed order:

[source,ts]
----
DISASTER_CHECK_ORDER = ['famine', 'flood', 'fire', 'plague', 'pirates', 'volcano', 'earthquake']
----

The first disaster that passes its trigger check, is not prevented, and rolls
under its chance **lands**; nothing else is tested for that city that turn.
Two consequences worth knowing:

* At most one disaster per city per turn — catastrophes never stack.
* The roll is deterministic under a stubbed `Math.random`, so the whole
  system is testable without seeding the game (`forceRandom(0)` makes every
  roll land; the first eligible disaster is the one that strikes).

The roll happens at the end of a city's turn processing: growth and the turn's
production have both landed, so the loss is felt on the turn it arrives, and
the store refresh at the end of `processTurnEvents` shows the new population,
the missing building and the emptied stores immediately. AI cities roll too —
a disaster is news for whoever owns the city; only the *notification* is
gated to the human player.

[[tuning]]
== Tuning: Far Rarer Than Civ1, a Little Harder

Chances are per eligible city per turn and live in one exported table:

[cols="2,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Disaster | Chance | Effect (Civ1 → here)

| Famine | 0.09% | −33% pop → −40%, food stores wiped
| Flood | 0.04% | −25% pop → −33% pop
| Fire | 0.08% | 1 building (as Civ1)
| Plague | 0.07% | −25% pop → −33% pop
| Pirates | 0.07% | gold stolen + stocks wiped
| Volcano | 0.04% | −33% pop → −40% + building
| Earthquake | 0.04% | 1 building
|===

The flood is the rarest on purpose: it was the Civ1 disaster the player
remembered as constant. In a ten-city empire a specific city sees a flood
about once every 2,500 turns; the empire as a whole, about once per 250 turns.

Population losses are `floor(population × share)`, at least one citizen and at
most `population − 1`. The dead citizens' tiles are released exactly the way
starvation releases them (specialists demoted first, then worked tiles), and
`foodNeeded` moves with the population so the city screen never shows a stale
growth target.

[[event]]
== The Event, the Toast and the Sound

`DisasterManager.strike` applies the effect and emits one event:

[source,ts]
----
this.gameEngine.onStateChange?.('CITY_DISASTER', {
  city,                 // the struck city (live reference, like CITY_STARVED)
  civilizationId,       // its owner
  kind,                 // 'famine' | 'flood' | 'fire' | 'plague' | 'pirates' | 'volcano' | 'earthquake'
  lostPopulation,       // citizens lost (0 for fire, earthquake, empty raids)
  destroyedBuilding,    // building id or null
  goldStolen,           // 0 unless pirates
});
----

`EngineEventHandlers.onCityDisaster` turns it into a toast for the owning
human player (`notify('warning', …, city.civilizationId)`, so hotseat seats
only see their own news), plays the `cityDisaster` cue through the same
`playNewsCue` gate as the other news cues, and refreshes the store. The
message is one `event.disaster.<kind>` string per disaster in all six
languages; the volcano has a second variant that names the burnt building when
it had one to burn.

== Where Things Live

* `src/game/engine/DisasterManager.ts` — the rules, chances, effects and the
  `CITY_DISASTER` emission.
* `src/game/engine/TurnManager.ts` — one `processCity` call per city at the
  end of its turn.
* `src/game/engine/GameEngine.ts` — `disasterManager` field, constructed with
  the other managers.
* `src/utils/EngineEventHandlers.ts` — `CITY_DISASTER` → toast + cue.
* `src/utils/Sfx.ts`, `src/utils/SoundEngine.ts` — the `cityDisaster` cue
  (one voice for all seven; a cue that fires once a game per kind would never
  stay recognisable).
* `src/i18n/{en,de,es,fr,ja,ru}.ts` — the `event.disaster.*` strings.
* `tests/disasters.test.ts` — triggers, prevention, effects, frequency
  invariants and a full-pipeline run.

The related replay-safe precedent is the meltdown: `EconomicManager` emits
`NUCLEAR_MELTDOWN` during `processTurn`, the router turns it into a toast,
news line and the `nuclearStrike` cue. Disasters follow the same shape, with
their own manager because seven events deserve their own file rather than a
seventh lump in a turn processor.
