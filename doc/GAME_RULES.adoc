= Game Rules Reference

The single authoritative source for *what the game actually does*.
Every number below was read out of the source, not out of an older document.

[IMPORTANT]
====
If you are changing a balance value, this is the file to update.
Source of truth per section is named in the "Defined in" note.
====

Where this document and `GAMEPLAY.md` disagree, *this document is right* and
`GAMEPLAY.md` is out of date.

== Conventions

* Stats are written `attack / defense / movement`.
* Gold costs are shields for units and buildings.
* `Def` in a terrain table is a *multiplier* on the defender's strength
  (`1.5` = +50 %).
* Technology `cost` is the *base* beaker cost. The real cost is scaled — see
  <<Research costs>>.

== The turn cycle

Defined in: `src/game/engine/TurnManager.ts`

=== Phases

Every civilization's turn runs the same five phases:

[cols="1,1,3", options="header"]
|===
|Phase |Constant |What happens

|1. START
|`START`
|Sets the active player, emits `TURN_START`. AI civilizations register themselves
immediately; the human player registers from the UI.

|2. UNIT MOVEMENT
|`UNIT_MOVEMENT`
|Queued GoTo paths walk first, then the unit turn queue is built and units act.
AI runs `runAIUnitMovementPhase`; the human gets `selectCurrentQueueUnit`.

|3. CITY PRODUCTION
|`CITY_PRODUCTION`
|Auto-production fills build queues for every city. Emits `CITY_PRODUCTION_PHASE`.

|4. RESEARCH
|`RESEARCH`
|AI without a current research picks one via `AIResearch.selectResearch`.
Banked science is spent on completion.

|5. END
|`END`
|Clears the unit queue, emits `TURN_END` with a per-civilization city dump, then
hands over to `advanceTurn`.
|===

=== At the round boundary

`advanceTurn()` does this, in order:

. Roll over into a new round if the next player is the first active player:
. `roundNumber += 1`
. `advanceYear()` — see <<The calendar>>
. `scoutMemory.setCurrentRound()`
. `barbarianManager.processBarbarians()` — the horde acts once per round,
before anybody else
. Log the round banner and push the turn/year to the store
. `diplomacyManager.processTurn(roundNumber)` — reputation recovery, treaty
income, mutual-defense joins
. `victoryManager.evaluateEndOfTurn()` — the game may end right here
. Hand over to the next civilization:
. `resetUnitsForPlayer(next)`
. `processTurnEvents(next)` — the whole economic pipeline, see below
. `startTurn(next)`

=== Unit reset

`resetUnitsForPlayer` runs per unit:

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
|Unit state |Result

|`isDefeated`
|Moves 0, `areTurnsDone`. A dead unit never comes back.

|Passenger of a ferry (`embarkedOn`)
|Moves 0, `areTurnsDone`. It resumes when unloaded.

|Fortified
|Moves 0, `areTurnsDone`. Fortification is never "called up" on its own.

|Asleep
|Refreshed moves, `areTurnsDone = true`.

|Building an improvement
|Refreshed moves, then `advanceUnitWork` — construction is multi-turn.

|Fishing an active route
|Refreshed moves, then `advanceFishing` — the fishing cycle is multi-turn.

|Normal
|Moves restored to the unit's full movement, `hasMovedThisTurn = false`.
|===

=== The economic pipeline

`processTurnEvents` order matters — yields are computed for *all* cities before
any city grows or produces:

. Deliver everything the city `purchase`d, and clear the "sold a building" flag
. **Pass 1 — yields:** `recomputeCityYields` then `applyCityOutputs` for every city
. **Pass 2 — growth and production:**
. Decrement `capturedTurns`; emit `CITY_DISORDER` only on the transition
. `processCityGrowth`
. `processCityProduction`
. `governmentManager.processTurn(civ)` — a revolution that just ended applies
*before* the economy is charged, so the new tax rate takes effect immediately
. `aiEconomicManager.preProcessTurn` then `economicManager.processTurn` — taxes,
science, luxury, upkeep, and the disband-if-broke pass
. `researchManager.advanceResearch`
. Push cities, civilizations and units to the store; emit `TURN_PROCESSED`

=== The calendar

The year advances once per round, never per turn.

* Game starts in *4000 BC* (`currentYear = -4000`).
* The year advances once per round, never per turn.
* Year `0` is skipped: 1 BC is followed by 1 AD.

[cols="1,1", options="header"]
|===
|From |Years per round

|Before 1000 AD
|+20

|1000 – 1499 AD
|+10

|1500 – 1749 AD
|+5

|1750 – 1849 AD
|+2

|1850 AD and later
|+1
|===

Roughly 250 rounds to reach 1850, ~370 to reach 2100.

[NOTE]
====
`GameConstants.TURNS_PER_YEAR = 10` still exists and `GameUtils.turnToYear`
still uses it, but it is *not* what drives the game. `turnToYear` is marked
`@deprecated` in favour of `TurnManager.advanceYear()`. Do not use
`TURNS_PER_YEAR` to reason about the calendar.
====

=== Auto end of turn

See link:auto-end-turn.adoc[Auto. turn ending] for the full rules. In short: a
unit is *active* when it has moves left and is not asleep, fortified, skipped or
done. The turn auto-ends for a human only when **no** unit is active **and** the
queue is empty **and** no manually-managed city still has a build queue or an
empty-but-buildable one.

== The map

=== The grid

* **8-way square grid**, not hexagonal. `src/game/SquareGrid.ts`.
* Default map is 80 × 50.
* Cities work a 5 × 5 block minus the centre and the four extreme corners —
  **20 workable tiles** (`isTileInCityRadius`).
* `MIN_CITY_CENTER_DISTANCE = 5` (Chebyshev) is enforced **for AI civilizations
  only**. A human may found adjacent cities.

=== Yields

Base yields above are the *terrain* yields. A tile's real yield is
terrain + improvement + special resource, and then the city centre gets its own
floor of **2 food / 1 production / 1 trade**, plus **+1 trade** if the city sits
on a river.

=== Effects

[cols="2,3,2", options="header"]
|===
|Improvement |Effect |Extra rules

|Irrigation
|+1 food
|Converts forest→plains, jungle→grassland, swamp→grassland. Needs orthogonally
adjacent fresh water (river or lake), or an already-irrigated neighbour.

|Road
|Movement cost × 1/3. +1 trade, but *only* on grassland, plains and desert
|Buildable almost anywhere.

|Railroad
|Movement cost 0.05. +0.5 food, +0.5 production, +0.5 trade
|Requires the `railroad` technology *and* an existing road.

|Mines
|+3 production on hills, +1 on mountains
|Converts grassland and plains into forest. Only desert, grassland, hills,
mountains and plains.

|Fortress
|Defence ×2
|Requires the `construction` technology. Healing is *not* implemented despite
the data comment.

|Pollution
|−1 food, −1 production, −1 trade, −1 health
|Not built by settlers. Removed by `cleanPollution`, which costs 2 movement
points.
|===

A road's +1 trade is a common misconception — it is a flat +1 on the three
tile types above, not a percentage of the tile's trade.

== Combat

Defined in: `src/game/engine/CombatSystem.ts` and `GameEngine.resolveCityCombat`

=== Strength

[source]
----
attackerStrength = attack × (health / 100)

defenderStrength = defense
                × (health / 100)
                × max(1, terrainDefence)
                × 1.5            if fortified, or the tile holds a friendly city
                × fortressMult   if the tile has a Fortress (×2)

winChance = attackerStrength / (attackerStrength + defenderStrength)
----

The roll is `random() × (A + D) < A`.

=== Damage

`COMBAT_DAMAGE = 25` — **only the loser takes damage.**

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
|Outcome |Damage

|Attacker wins, and outguns the defender (`A >= D`) or the defender is already at 25 HP or less
|Destroyed outright.

|Attacker wins otherwise
|Defender takes 25 HP.

|Defender wins
|Attacker takes 25 HP. The defender takes nothing.
|===

=== Cities

`cityDefense = max(1, population)`, **× 3 with city walls**.

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
|Rule |Detail

|Garrison
|Garrison units die **one per round**. There is no "all defenders die at once"
rule. The city only falls once the garrison is empty.

|Walls
|Walls are ignored by air units, Cannons and Artillery.

|Fall
|Population 1 or less → the city is *razed*. Otherwise population −1 and the
city is captured.

|Aftermath
|Walls are always destroyed, plus 1 random non-Palace/non-wonder building per
population point lost. The whole garrison is wiped. Production is reset.
`capturedTurns = 5` — unrest decays 1 per round.

|Plunder
|The attacker takes `min(floor(treasury × 0.2), 100)`.

|Capital
|A captured capital loses its Palace; the old owner gets a new capital assigned.
|===

=== Other combat rules

* Attacking **auto-declares war**, and the war cascades to the defender's allies.
* The attacker never advances into the defeated unit's tile automatically.
* A dead unit is removed after a 1.5 s delay so the death animation can play.
  It is already invisible to every lookup.
* Destroying a transport **sinks its whole cargo**.
* Civilians (settler, worker, caravan, diplomat) cannot attack or capture cities.
  A caravan may still *enter* an enemy city to deliver.
* A Scout may enter an **undefended** enemy city with a 30 % chance. Failure
  rejects the move.

== Movement

=== Costs

* Terrain cost from the <<Terrains>> table: 1, 2, or 3 for mountains.
* Road × 1/3, Railroad 0.05.
* *Minimum 1 move*: a unit that has not moved yet this turn may always enter one
  adjacent tile even if it costs more than it has left. That move spends
  everything it has.
* Moving breaks fortification and wakes the unit. Abandoning a construction site
  cancels the work.

=== Rivers

Defined in: `src/game/engine/RiverRules.ts`

* A river tile is fordable if the river run through it is 1 tile wide.
* A river **2 or more wide in both axes** is impassable to land units from every
  direction. Ships still navigate it.
* `MAX_FORDABLE_RIVER_WIDTH = 1`.
* Lakes are impassable to everyone, ships included.

=== Where ships may go

Ocean, a navigable river, or **their own** city that has naval access. Nothing
else.

=== Zones of control

A settler may not enter a tile adjacent to an enemy military unit with attack
above 0.

== Fishing

Defined in: `src/game/engine/FisherEconomics.ts`

A Fisher Boat needs a Harbor building and then sails a repeating cycle out to a
fish tile and back.

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
|Quantity |Formula

|Cycle length
|`6 + 2 × ceil(distance / movement)` turns

|Food per fish
|`min(3, 1 + floor(distance / 4))` — 1, 2 or 3

|Catch value
|`6 × food per fish` — 6, 12 or 18 food

|Food per turn
|`catch value / cycle length`

|Net value per turn
|`(food per turn + net tile food) × 2`, minus upkeep

|Worth building
|Only if net value per turn > 0

|Overhead
|`maintenance + cost / 20` per turn
|===

Without an active net, a Fish tile pays **1 less food** than its printed yield.
Overflow that does not fit in the food box grants a one-off +2 food.

Only one Fisher Boat per city, and one net at a time.

== Trade routes

A caravan walks to a friendly or foreign city and opens a route.

[source]
----
payout       = max(1, round((homePop + destPop) × (1 + distance / 4) × multiplier))
multiplier   = 1 × (foreign ? 2 : 1) × (intercontinental ? 2 : 1)
routeTrade   = max(1, round((homePop + destPop) / 4))
----

* Payout arrives as **both gold and science**.
* *Intercontinental* means a Bresenham line between the two cities crosses
  ocean or sea. A lake does not count.
* Maximum **3** trade routes per civilization. A new route replaces the weakest.
* The caravan is consumed on delivery.
* `MAX_CARAVAN_TRADE_ROUTES = 3`.

== The economy

Defined in: `src/game/engine/EconomicManager.ts`

=== Commerce

[source]
----
commerce             = max(tileTrade, CITY_CENTER_COMMERCE=2) + Σ tradeRoutes
afterGovPenalty      = commerce × (1 − government.commercePenalty)
corruption           = floor(afterGovPenalty × corruptionRate)
afterCorruption      = max(0, floor(afterGovPenalty − corruption))

tax                  = floor(afterCorruption × taxRate / 100)  × 2
science              = round(afterCorruption × scienceRate / 100)
                       + buildingScience + specialistScience
luxury               = floor(afterCorruption × luxuryRate / 100)
----

Tax rate, science rate and luxury rate must sum to 100.

=== Corruption

[source]
----
distance     = euclidean distance from the capital
distanceMult = min(distance / 10, 2)
rate         = 0.30 × distanceMult          <- hard-coded base, not government-based
if courthouse: rate = rate / 2
corruption   = floor(trade × rate)
----

The capital itself is never corrupt. A Courthouse halves the rate. Note the base
`0.30` is hard-coded in `CityUtils.calculateCorruption` and does **not** read
`government.corruptionRate` — the government table's corruption column is
currently only used by the AI's own reasoning.

=== Food and growth

[source]
----
-- Each citizen eats 2 food a turn to begin with, and 0.2 MORE for every
-- city size above 5. A size-6 citizen eats 2.2, a size-12 citizen 3.4.
perCitizen  = 2 + 0.2 × max(0, population − 5)

consumption = population × perCitizen
           + settlers × (1, or 2 under Republic/Democracy)
surplus     = produced − consumption

foodStored += surplus
threshold  = (population + 1) × 10
----

The growing appetite is the point: a large city is genuinely harder to keep fed
than a small one, so a civ is pushed to keep expanding while it can and is
punished for letting one city grow fat and isolated. It is also what turns a
starvation into a *siege* — see <<Blockading a city>> below, where a city that
loses access to its fields dies at this rate rather than a flat one.

`perCitizen` is exported as `citizenFoodDemand` from `EconomicManager` and is
the single source of truth: the growth pipeline, the AI's food planning and the
shared tile assigner all charge the same rate, so a famine can never be priced
at a different appetite than the citizen is assumed to eat at.

[cols="2,4", options="header"]
|===
|Event |Result

|Growth
|Food stored ≥ threshold, and the city is **not** in disorder. Population +1.
The food box is emptied — unless there is a Granary, in which case
`floor(stored / 2)` is kept.

|Starvation
|Food stored < 0. Population −1 and the box is reset to 0. Disorder does **not**
prevent starvation. At population 0 the city is destroyed.

|Size 1 and a settler completes
|The city is **destroyed** and the settler becomes a NONE unit with no home
city — unless the difficulty is Chieftain.
|===

There is **no** housing cap. An Aqueduct does not gate city size; the AI only
treats it as a growth prerequisite in its own planning.

[[Blockading a city]]
=== Blockading a city

A city works the tiles around it. **A tile occupied by a unit of another
civilization is not worked**, for as long as that unit stands there.

[source]
----
tile is usable  ⇔  no live foreign unit stands on it
-----

The tile is handed back the moment the unit leaves, so the effect is a *race*:
every turn a defender is parked on a field is a turn of food the city does not
get. Applied at all four points where a tile can be worked or offered:

* the automatic worker assignment never picks a blocked tile;
* a blocked tile is not offered in the *Available Tiles* list;
* a manual assignment onto it is refused;
* a tile the player had already assigned **stops paying immediately** rather
  than waiting for the next automatic re-pick.

A unit of the city's **own** civilization never blocks anything — garrisons,
settlers and builders standing on the fields they feed is the ordinary case.

Because the city's appetite rises with size (§ <<Food and growth>>), the same
blockade kills a big city considerably faster than a small one. This is the
mechanic the AI uses offensively: `findBlockadeAssignment` sends a unit to
squat on the richest field an enemy city works, which costs one unit and never
risks losing it in an assault the AI cannot win.


=== Happiness and disorder

[source]
----
unhappiness = max(0, population − government.tolerance)
             + 3 if the city is freshly captured

happiness   = luxury + specialistLuxury + martialLaw
            + buildingHappiness + government.happinessBonus
            + 2 (base contentment)

disorder    = unhappiness > happiness
----

* Martial law can suppress up to 4 in Despotism and Anarchy, 3 in Monarchy and
  Communism, and **1** in Republic and Democracy.
* A unit garrisons a city by standing on its tile or by being fortified on or
  beside it; an unfortified unit that is merely nearby does not count.
* A disordered city produces **no** tax, science, luxury or commerce.
* `CAPTURED_CITY_UNHAPPY = 3`.

=== Bankruptcy

If gold goes negative:

. Emergency tax — tax to maximum, science and luxury to 0, re-accumulate.
. Still short → disband units, in this order:
. units that are not the only defender of their city
. non-scouts
. descending maintenance
. descending cost
. A second pass breaks the last-defender rule if it must.
. Treasuries are never dropped below `ABSOLUTE_MIN_GOLD = 8`.
. A Fisher Boat on an *active* route is never a disband candidate.

=== Research costs

[source]
----
mapTechRate        = clamp(round((mapWidth + mapHeight) / 45), 1, 3)
difficultyFactor   = CHIEFTAIN 1.2 | WARLORD 1.1 | PRINCE 1.0 | KING 0.9 | EMPEROR 0.8
techCountFactor    = clamp(1 + (myTechs − maxTechs) × 0.1, 0.8, 1.2)

effectiveCost      = max(10, floor(baseCost × mapTechRate / difficultyFactor
                                        × techCountFactor))

knownCivsModifier  = clamp(1 − knownCivs × 0.1, 0.6, 1)
prereqModifier     = clamp(1 − (missingPrereqs / totalPrereqs) × 0.4, 0.6, 1)
beakers            = floor(floor((baseCost + 1) × knownCivsModifier) × prereqModifier)

perTurnProgress    = clamp(beakers, ceil(effectiveCost / 32), remaining)
----

* A **larger map is harder**: the tech rate scales with map size.
* A **higher difficulty is easier**: the factor divides, and Emperor is 0.8.
* `MIN_RESEARCH_TURNS = 4`, `MAX_RESEARCH_TURNS = 32` per technology.
* Research is locked for the **first 5 rounds**.
* Science produced with nothing selected banks in `civ.bankedScience` and is
  spent as soon as a technology is chosen.
* Completing Metallurgy scraps every city wall in the empire.

=== The settler shield tax

Under Republic and Democracy, each of your own settlers **costs the city 1
shield** per turn. Every other government pays nothing. This is the main reason
Democracy is hard for an expansionist, and it is the real cost of a settler —
not the 0 upkeep in the unit table.

== Barbarians

Defined in: `src/game/engine/BarbarianManager.ts`

* Barbarians are a **phantom civilization** with id `BARBARIAN_CIV_ID = -1`. They
  are excluded from the turn rotation and act once per round, before anyone
  else.
* They only become a real faction — a civilization you can see, score, and must
  destroy — the moment they **hold a city**. Until then they cannot be a loss
  condition.
* Horde strength is `Σ(max(0.5, attack) + defense × 0.3)`.
* An assault needs at least 2 units and `hordeStrength >= cityDefense × 1.5`.
* `estimateCityDefense = max(1, population) × (3 with walls) + Σ(attack + defense × 0.5)`
  for units within Chebyshev distance 2.
* A captured barbarian city has all its buildings sold, switches to
  auto-production, and pumps out raiders once per round.

== Villages (goody huts)

Defined in: `src/data/VillageConstants.ts`

* Density: one per 150 tiles, minimum 1 per map.
* Never on ocean, mountains or a river tile. Never on a special-resource tile.
  Never within Chebyshev 2 of a starting settler.
* Only **land** units trigger a village. Air units fly over.
* The hut is consumed the instant it is triggered.

Five outcomes, **20 % each**:

[cols="2,4", options="header"]
|===
|Outcome |Effect

|Advanced Tribe
|Only for a settler, and not on a tile next to a city. Founds a size-1 city with
a random free building from Barracks, Granary, Temple.

|Scroll of Ancient Wisdom
|A free technology.

|Valuable Metals
|25, 50 or 100 gold — equal chance.

|Friendly Mercenaries
|A settler-triggered hut gives a Settler with a NONE home city. Any other land
unit gets the strongest buildable military unit.

|Barbarian Ambush
|1 to 3 Warriors or Legions on free adjacent tiles. Each one **attacks the
triggering unit in the same turn**.
|===

An invalid roll is re-rolled up to 10 times, then falls back to gold.

=== Reputation

[source]
----
attitude = (personality.diplomacy − 5) × 3
         − (personality.aggression − 5) × 2
         − brokenTreaties × 15
         + reputationModifier
         + statusBonus        (+20 alliance, −30 war, −10 ceasefire)
         + treatyBonus        (trade +5, open borders +3, mutual defence +8,
                               non-aggression +4)
         + strengthModifier   (−10 if the enemy is 1.5× stronger,
                               +5 if you are 2× stronger)
         + borderFriction     (−8 within 4 tiles, −3 within 7)
----

Bands: `≥ 15` friendly, `≥ −5` neutral, `≥ −20` annoyed, else hostile.

Penalties: ceasefire cooldown 5 turns, peace broken −30, alliance broken −50,
surprise attack −20. Reputation recovers 1 per turn.

=== Willingness

Base by attitude: friendly 75, neutral 50, annoyed 30, hostile 10. Then treaty
modifiers — peace +15, ceasefire +20, alliance −10, tribute −20 (+25 if you are
1.5× stronger), open borders +5, trade +10, mutual defence −15, non-aggression
+15, embargo −10, tech exchange +5. Minus 15 per treaty broken. Clamped 0–100.

=== Bribery

Cost is `25 × (attack + defense)`, success chance 60 % — +20 % against a hostile
target, −20 % against a friendly one. A success transfers the unit **and
auto-declares war**. The diplomat is consumed.

== Victory

Defined in: `src/game/engine/VictoryManager.ts`

There are exactly **two** win conditions and no third fallback.

[cols="2,4", options="header"]
|===
|Condition |Detail

|Moonshot
|Any civilization that completes Moonshot wins immediately.

|Elimination
|Only one civilization is still *operational* — it owns at least one unit or one
city.
|===

[IMPORTANT]
====
There is deliberately **no** "capture every city" shortcut, and **no**
score-based win at 2100 AD. A rival with one surviving unit keeps the game
running.

The human loses when their civilization is eliminated, or when an AI wins while
the human is still alive.

On any result the engine sets `isGameOver` and `setPaused(true)`, which freezes
all AI and turn logic.
====

=== Score

Recomputed every turn end, so the scoreboard and the exported CSV stay live.

[source]
----
score = population
      + unique workable tiles in your cities' radii
      + number of cities
      + cities × 5
      + technologies × 5
      + wonders × 20
      + floor(consecutivePeaceRounds / 5)
      − pollution from your buildings
----

`peaceTurns` increments each round at peace and resets to 0 when you go to war.
