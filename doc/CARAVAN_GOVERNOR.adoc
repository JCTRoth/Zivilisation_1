= Caravan Governor — Structured Analysis and Redesign

Analysis of `src/game/engine/AI/CaravanGovernor.ts` (the per-unit caravan brain)
and its civ-level partner `src/game/engine/AI/CaravanCoordinator.ts`, plus the
redesign that follows from it.

== 1. What the measured game says

The whole system produces **zero trade routes**. A 150-round probe on
`AI_VS_AI` (4 civs, seed 7) measured:

[cols="1,1,2", options="header"]
|===
|Stage |Count |Where it dies

|Caravans produced |**0** |`AutoProduction.shouldBuildCaravan` is gated on `canAffordAnotherUnit` (a gold test) even though a caravan costs 0 gold/turn, and on `canBuildUnit(civ,'caravan')` → the `trade` tech
|Research hook |fires |`caravanRelevanceForCiv` now pushes `bronze_working → currency → trade`
|Deliveries |0 |nothing to deliver
|Improved tiles |24 |(fixed separately — the works corps)
|===

So the governor is not the current bottleneck; production is. But the governor
has structural problems of its own that would cap the delivery rate even with a
steady supply of caravans, and those are what this document analyses.

== 2. The governor as it stands — five concepts

. **Sticky missions.** A target is chosen once and kept until delivered,
   ineligible, or `CARAVAN_BLOCK_PATIENCE` turns without a route. This exists
   because the pre-governor chooser re-scored every turn and the caravan paced
   back and forth. Correct, and it stays.
. **Risk-taking routing.** The plain A* route is taken even past enemies; a
   safer alternate to the SAME target is only used when one exists and clearly
   reduces the risk. Correct in spirit.
. **Blacklist + patience.** A target the caravan cannot reach is written off per
   unit. Correct.
. **One scout per civ.** `CARAVAN_MAX_SCOUTS = 1`, with hand-off when the holder
   dies. Correct.
. **Surplus cap.** `MAX_CARAVANS_PER_CITY` in-transit caravans per home city; the
   newest above the cap are disbanded. Correct.

== 3. The seven structural problems

=== P1 — Value is scored per *trip*, not per *turn*

`pickDeliveryTarget` scores
`(homePop + destPop) × (1 + dist/4) × (foreign ? 2 : 1)`, so **distance
multiplies value**. For a unit with movement 1 that is exactly backwards: a city
8 tiles away is worth the same as one 2 tiles away but takes 4× the turns. The
governor's own test pins this (the far city B is preferred over the near city A).

The correct unit of account is **value per turn of travel**.

=== P2 — One tile per turn, roads or not

`routePlan` returns `path[1]` — a single step. A caravan on a roaded tile costs
1/3 movement and could cross three tiles a turn; on a railroad, twenty. The
governor never uses its movement budget, so a road network (the thing the works
corps now builds) does nothing for trade.

=== P3 — No notion of route *slots* being worth taking

`isEligibleTarget` refuses a city whose `MAX_CARAVAN_TRADE_ROUTES` slots are
full. But the engine now evicts the **oldest** route when a new one arrives, so
a full city is still a good destination when the route being evicted is weaker
than the new one. The governor cannot see the difference between "full of great
routes" and "full of junk".

=== P4 — Threat is an undifferentiated blob

`buildDangerMap` puts every enemy unit's whole 8-neighbourhood into one set, so
an enemy on the far side of the world contributes the same risk as one standing
on the route. There is no distance weighting, no notion of whether the enemy can
catch a caravan, and no way to say *postpone*: the only answers are "detour" and
"take the risk anyway".

=== P5 — No transit deadline

A mission to a city 30 tiles away has no time limit. With `CARAVAN_BLOCK_PATIENCE`
only counting *no-route* turns, a caravan can spend 40 turns walking and the
governor has no opinion about it.

=== P6 — No escort concept

Caravans are defenceless (attack 0, defense 0). The governor's answer to danger
is routing. There is no notion of "wait for a friendly warship", "travel in
company", or "wait until the war ends" beyond the hard at-war filter.

=== P7 — No civ-level ledger

Missions are keyed by unit id and nothing aggregates them. There is no answer to
"how long has this civ's caravans been in transit on average", "how many
deliveries did it ever make", or "is the caravan fleet productive". The
coordinator partly fills this, but it recomputes from scratch every round and
keeps no history.

=== P8 — `planUnit` is one 150-line method

Five phases inline, four private helpers, and the lifecycle as a bare string
kind. It is the reason P1–P7 are expensive to fix: there is nowhere to put a new
concept.

== 4. The redesign

Kept: the public API (`planUnit`, `assignTarget`, `getMission`, `getScoutHolder`,
`forget`, `pruneMissing`, `caravansOfCity`), the sticky mission, the
risk-taking bias, the blacklist, the scout role and the surplus cap. All of the
existing behaviour tests still pass.

Added, as first-class concepts:

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|Concept |What it is |Why

|`MissionPhase`
|`outbound → delivering → delivered` with explicit `abortReason`, replacing the bare `'wait' | 'scout' | 'idle'` kind
|A lifecycle is what a deadline, an escort wait and an abort reason need to hang from

|`RouteValuation`
|`routeValue`, `routeTrade`, `travelTurns`, `valuePerTurn`, `slotGain`
|Routes are ranked by value per TURN, and a full-slot destination is only taken when the evicted route is weaker

|`TravelBudget`
|The furthest tile along the path the unit can actually pay for this turn
|Roads (1/3) and railroads (~1/20) now shorten deliveries

|`ThreatProfile`
|Enemy units near the route weighted by distance and by whether they can catch a caravan, plus a `postpone` answer
|"Detour or die" becomes "detour, wait for an escort, or take it"

|`EscortRequest`
|A caravan marks itself as wanting an escort while a hostile sits on its route and a friendly hull is within reach
|The first use of the navy that is not a warship race

|`CivLedger`
|Per civ: in-flight, deliveries, total transit turns, last delivery round
|Makes the fleet measurable and gives the coordinator history

|`MAX_TRANSIT_TURNS`
|A mission deadline, after which the target is blacklisted
|A caravan that has walked for 40 turns stops being a delivery and becomes a waste
|===

== 5. Implemented

All of the above is now in `src/game/engine/AI/CaravanGovernor.ts`, with the new
balance numbers in `src/data/GameConstants.ts` and the behaviour pinned by
`tests/caravanGovernor.test.ts` (21 cases) and `tests/ai/tradeAndWorks.test.ts`.

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|Concept |Where |Pinned by

|`MissionPhase` (`outbound → delivering → delivered`, `scouting`, `idle`)
|`CaravanMission.phase` replaces the old `kind` string
|`getMission(...).phase` assertions throughout

|`RouteValuation` (`valuePerTurn`, `slotGain`, `travelTurns`)
|`valueRoute()`, consumed by `pickDeliveryTarget()`
|"ranks a route by value per TURN, not per trip"; "DOES take a route slot away from a full city when the new route is better"

|`TravelBudget` (`stepWithinBudget`)
|`routePlan()` returns the furthest tile the unit can pay for, so a roaded
caravan covers three tiles a turn
|"covers several tiles in one step when the terrain is cheap"

|`ThreatProfile` (weighted, d ≤ 1 only)
|`riskOf()` — an enemy two tiles out cannot catch a movement-1 caravan and now
contributes nothing; `dangerAt()` keeps the tight pathing radius
|the three risk tests, including the safer-alternate one

|`EscortRequest`
|`routePlan()` waits `CARAVAN_ESCORT_PATIENCE` turns when a risky route has a
friendly hull within `CARAVAN_ESCORT_RADIUS`
|escort constants + the escort branch

|`CivLedger`
|`getLedger(civId)`: in-flight, deliveries, transit turns, aborts
|"keeps a per-civ ledger of what the fleet did"

|`CARAVAN_MAX_TRANSIT_TURNS`
|`routePlan()` abandons a mission that has walked too long
|"abandons a mission that has taken too long to arrive"
|===

The one thing deliberately NOT changed: `planUnit` is still the single entry
point, but every phase is now a named private step with one job, and the
lifecycle is a union type instead of a bare string — which is what made the new
concepts cheap to add.

== 6. What still blocks deliveries

Two silent turn-killers, found by instrumenting a real engine (and now pinned by
`tests/ai/caravanPlannerRealEngine.test.ts`, which runs a REAL engine through a
full AI turn and asserts nothing was reported). Neither produced a failing test;
both produced a civ that *quietly stopped playing*, because the AI turn's own
catch swallows any error and the turn still ends normally.

. `world.isAtWar is not a function` — the coordinator's world is
   `{ cities, units, isAtWar, hasContacted }`, and the GameEngine exposes none of
   the last two: diplomacy lives on `diplomacyManager`. The engine was cast
   straight in, so **every AI turn that had a caravan threw**, aborted, and issued
   no orders at all. A 150-round probe logged that one error 967,607 times.
   Fixed by an explicit adapter naming the two questions the planner may ask
   (`AIManager.coordinateCaravans`).
. `blacklisted is not iterable` — `CaravanGovernor.assignTarget` (the
   coordinator's hook) handed `null` into `freshMission`, which spreads its
   blacklist. Same consequence: the turn dies the moment a caravan is assigned.

A third, quieter bug in the same place: `assignTarget`'s sticky guard was
inverted, so it re-targeted every walking caravan **every round** — the exact
pacing the sticky missions exist to prevent. It now only overrides on `force`.

A fourth, in the economy rather than the AI: the upkeep disbander used to find
caravans only by name. Now any unit that costs 0 gold a turn is exempt — removing
it cannot cure an upkeep deficit, so it was destroying 50 shields and a road the
civ was still earning from to save nothing. Settlers were the remaining case.

The lesson the docs should carry: the AI turn is one try/catch around a long
pipeline, so a throw anywhere disables an entire civ for the rest of that turn,
and the game shows no symptom. The whole of a civ's behaviour — moving, attacking,
delivering — disappears behind a silent abort, which is why the two fixes above are
pinned by a test that runs a **real engine** through a full turn and asserts that
nothing was reported.
