= City Capture & Destruction

Civ I–style: military units that step onto an enemy city tile capture (or, at
size 1, raze) the city. Both the *human* and the *AI* use the same engine
path (`GameEngine.moveUnit` → `resolveCityCombat`).

== Mechanics (`GameEngine.resolveCityCombat`)

* *Attacker strength* = `attack × (health / 100) × rankMultiplier`
  (`CombatSystem.attackerStrength`, `CombatSystem.ts:150-152`; rank runs from
  Rookie ×1 to Battle-Hardened ×1.5) — civilians with attack 0 have
  zero strength, so they cannot take a city.
* *City defense* = `max(1, population)` × *1.5* when `city_walls` stands
  (`defense: 2` in `BuildingConstants.ts:126` → `1 + 2 × 0.25` in
  `BuildingEffects.ts:147-148`, applied in `CombatSystem.resolveCityRound`,
  `CombatSystem.ts:253-270`) — but air units
  (fighters/bombers) and siege artillery (cannon/artillery, the Howitzer
  stand-ins) *ignore the walls entirely*.
* Win roll: `Math.random() * (attack + defense) < attack`.
* *Win vs pop > 1* → city captured, then the full Civ I aftermath runs:
  . *Population −1* and `civilizationId` switches to the attacker.
  . *Building destruction* — city walls NEVER survive a capture; one more
     random non-palace, non-wonder building is destroyed per population lost.
     Wonders and the Palace survive (a captured capital still loses its Palace
     and `GovernmentManager.ensureCapital` designates a replacement).
  . *Gold plundering* — 20% of the defender's treasury (capped at 100) is
     transferred to the capturer.
  . *Plundered knowledge* — the conqueror's scientists take ONE technology the
     former owner knew and the conqueror did not (`plunderTechOnCapture`, which
     draws from `DiplomacyManager.getTechTradeOptions().theyHaveWeLack`). The
     pick is blind: no UI chooses it. `CITY_CAPTURED` carries `techId` /
     `techName` so the capture screen can announce it, and a razed city
     carries none — there are no libraries left to read.
  . *Garrison destroyed* — every unit of the old owner standing on the city
     tile is wiped out.
  . *Production reset* — `currentProduction`, `buildQueue`, and stored
     progress are cleared.
  . *Unrest* — the city gets `capturedTurns = 5`; captured citizens count as
     extra unhappy until the penalty fades, so fresh captures tend toward
     disorder unless garrisoned/managed.
  . The attacker unit is consumed and `CITY_CAPTURED` fires.
* *Win vs pop == 1* → city is *razed* (removed), garrison destroyed, attacker
  consumed, `CITY_DESTROYED` event (capital handling as above).
* *Loss* → attacker takes 25 damage (may be defeated), city keeps its owner —
  but an *unwalled* city has a 50% chance to lose a citizen to the failed
  attack; city walls fully shield the population.
* *Instant capture after clearing the garrison* — in `combatUnit`, when the
  last defender on an enemy city tile falls, the city is captured immediately
  (a garrisoned city can no longer be left permanently untouchable).
* Civilian block (`civilian_cannot_attack_city`, `GameEngine.ts:5031-5054`):
  `settler`, `worker` and `diplomat` cannot attack or capture a city — but
  `caravan` sits in the same set *and is excluded from the test*
  (`&& unit.type !== 'caravan'`, so it is never turned away by this rule), and
  `scout` is not blocked at all: it may *rush* an undefended city with a 30 %
  chance. `worker` never actually matches anything — there is no `worker` unit
  type in `UNIT_TYPES` (`src/data/UnitConstants.ts:25-71`), so in practice only
  settlers and diplomats are turned away.
* Attacking a city auto-declares war if the two civs are not already at war.

== AI behaviour

The AI already targeted cities reactively (its 5-tile enemy scan includes cities
and `moveUnit` handles the capture), but two gaps made it dormant in real games:

. *No AI personality on engine civs* — engine civs are plain objects and never
   got a `personality`, so `DiplomacyManager.processAIDiplomacy` always fell back
   to an all-5 profile → the AI could *never declare war on its own*.
   Fix: `AITypes.getCivPersonality(profile)` maps each production profile to a
   deterministic personality; `GameEngine.createCivilizations` now assigns it
   (e.g. `military_expansion` → aggression 8, `defensive_turtle` → aggression 2).
. *War-for-conquest declaration* — the old profile thresholds
   (`military_expansion ≥ 1.6×`, `balanced_growth ≥ 2.0×`) are gone.
   `DiplomacyManager.processAIDiplomacy` now scores a `declare_war` candidate in
   `AIDiplomacyPolicy` (`src/game/engine/diplomacy/AIDiplomacyPolicy.ts:478-500`),
   which needs *both*:

   * the `mayDeclareWar(ctx)` gate (`AIDiplomacyPolicy.ts:201-219`): war
     capacity (`activeWars < maxWars`), no independent second front for a team
     already at war, war exhaustion below 18, and at least 8 rounds of peace
     since the last one;
   * a target worth taking:
     `targetWorth = max(0, strengthRatio − warTargetMargin(year)) × 25`, where
     the margin *closes as the calendar runs out* — 1.6 before year 0, 1.4
     from year 0, 1.25 from year 1000, 1.1 from year 1500
     (`AIDiplomacyPolicy.ts:174-180`).

   The score is then multiplied by war appetite × temperament (a
   `defensive_turtle` temperament can reach zero, so it never opens a war of
   conquest at all) minus war fatigue, grievances and fear, plus
   `lateGameWarPush` (`AIDiplomacyPolicy.ts:187-195`). The chosen candidate is
   executed by the `declare_war` case in `DiplomacyManager.processAIDiplomacy`
   (`DiplomacyManager.ts:2613-2635`), which calls the allies in first and then
   declares.

Once at war, the AI captures cities via the existing pipeline: scout-discovered
enemy cities feed `enemyLocations` → `updateOffensivePlan` assigns combat units →
they march onto the city tile → `resolveCityCombat`.

== Last city lost — civilization eliminated

Civ I rule, implemented in `GameEngine.applyLastCityRule` (called from
`markCityLost`, which every city loss funnels through — capture, raze, scout
rush, nuclear strike):

* When a civilization loses its *last* city and it still has at least one unit
  on the board that is *not* a settler, every one of its remaining units is
  destroyed and `civ.isAlive` is set to `false`. A people without a city that
  still carries spears is a raider band, not a nation.
* The *settler exception*: a civilization left with nothing but settlers is
  spared. They can walk to open ground and found a new city, so their story is
  not over. `VictoryManager` then keeps them alive via its normal
  "has units or cities" check.
* Nothing happens if the civilization still owns a city somewhere else, or if
  it has no units left at all.

The engine emits `CIVILIZATION_ELIMINATED` with `{ civilizationId,
attackerCivId, unitsDestroyed }`. `EngineEventRouter.onCivilizationEliminated`
turns it into a toast (`NotificationCenter`):

* the *victim* hears an `error` toast ("… is no more — your last city has
  fallen, and your scattered units are destroyed with it."),
* the *conqueror* hears a `success` toast,
* anyone else hears an `info` toast naming the destroying civilization.

Toast text is suppressed entirely in AI-vs-AI mode (no human is watching).

== Events

`CITY_ATTACKED` / `CITY_CAPTURED` / `CITY_DESTROYED` — consumed by
`AutoProduction` (re-picks production for the affected civs), `GameLogger`,
`GameProgression` and `CitySnapshots`.

`CITY_CAPTURED` carries `{ city, capturedBy, originalCiv, techId?, techName? }`.
`originalCiv` is read *before* `resolveCityCombat` runs, because the capture
call flips `city.civilizationId` over to the attacker — the capture screen needs
the *former* owner's colour to lower the right banner.

`CIVILIZATION_ELIMINATED` — see the section above; consumed by
`EngineEventRouter` (toast) and `VictoryManager` (via `isAlive`).

== Capture screen

`CityEventModal` (dialog ids `city-founded` / `city-lost`, notice kind
`captured`) renders the conquest as a banner coming down and a bill being read
out:

* *The lowered banner* — `.city-event-flag` draws a pole with the former
  owner's colour (`civ.color`) and device (`civ.icon`), animated down the pole
  by `city-event-flag-lowered` (1500 ms, honoured `prefers-reduced-motion`).
  The whole thing carries an `aria-label` naming the flag and the city.
* *The plundered knowledge* — `.city-event-plunder` shows the engine's pick at
  the bottom of the window: "Our scientists plunder the city libraries and
  discover the secrets of X!". It only appears when the capture actually
  produced a tech, and never for a razed city.

== Tests

* `tests/cityCapture.test.ts` — capture, raze, capital re-establishment,
  civilian block, weak-attacker loss, walls ×1.5 defense, air units ignoring
  walls, building destruction on capture, gold plundering, garrison destruction,
  production reset + unrest flag, instant capture after clearing the garrison,
  failed-attack population loss vs wall protection, wall obsolescence, and the
  happiness unrest penalty — plus an end-to-end AI test where the AI declares
  war on its own and captures the city in one turn.
* `tests/lastCityElimination.test.ts` — the last-city rule: a non-settler unit
  in the field wipes every remaining unit (event payload, single `UNIT_REMOVED`
  sync, `isAlive` cleared, conqueror toast); settlers-only are spared; a second
  city suppresses the wipe; a size-1 raze triggers it too; and the victim's own
  toast fires when the human loses their last city.
* `tests/ai/DiplomacyManager.test.ts` — "AI war for conquest" (military_expansion
  declares war when strong; defensive_turtle does not).
* `tests/ai/productionProfiles.test.ts` — personalities match production profiles.
