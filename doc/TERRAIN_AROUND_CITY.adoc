= Terrain Around City — How It Works

== Overview

The terrain system around cities is the backbone of the economy. Every city claims a *diamond-shaped workable radius* of 20 tiles, and each tile yields Food, Production, and Trade based on its terrain type, special resources, and improvements. Citizens ("workers") are automatically assigned to the best available tiles, and the accumulated yields drive city growth, unit production, gold income, and research.

---

== 1. City Work Radius

Defined in `GameEngine.isTileInCityRadius()` (`src/game/engine/GameEngine.ts:4029`)
and `EconomicManager.cityTerritory()` (`src/game/engine/EconomicManager.ts:674`):

* The radius is a *Chebyshev distance ≤ 2* from the city center — a 5×5 square.
* The *4 extreme corners* of the 5×5 square are excluded (distance 2 in both axes).
* The *city center* itself is excluded from the radius list but is always worked.
* Result: *20 workable tiles* surrounding the city, plus the city center.

----

        . . . . .
        . . X . .    X = city center
        . . . . .
        . . . . .
    (corners removed → diamond shape)

== 3. Special Resources

Each terrain type can have *one special resource*. The chance a tile carries it
is set in `src/data/TerrainConstants.ts` (`rollResource`, applied by
`src/game/engine/MapGenerator/MapGeneratorHelper.ts:188`):

* default *0.15* per tile (`DEFAULT_RESOURCE_SPAWN_CHANCE`),
* ocean *0.18* (`COASTAL_FISH_CHANCE`), and only for water next to land — a
  deep-ocean tile gets no fish at all (`COASTAL_FISH_BONUS_CHANCE = 0`),
* river *0.09* (the ocean rate × `RIVER_FISH_CHANCE_MULTIPLIER` 0.5).

base yields (`getResourceYields`, `TerrainConstants.ts:359`, added in
`EconomicManager.tileYields`). Doubling exists only in the AI's settlement
*scoring* (see section 9, Settlement Placement, below):

== 4. Tile Improvements

Improvements are declared in `src/data/TileImprovementConstants.ts`
(`IMPROVEMENT_PROPERTIES`) and applied tile by tile in
`EconomicManager.tileYields` (`EconomicManager.ts:590-608`). Almost everything
is *per terrain*: a base `effects` bag plus `effectsByTerrain` /
`tradeBonusTerrains` overrides, and the build time comes from `turnsByTerrain`
(`GameEngine.canBuildImprovement`, `GameEngine.ts:8466-8470`: a missing entry
means the improvement cannot be built on that terrain).

*Terrain conversions* (Jungle/Swamp → Grassland, Forest → Plains, …) change the
base terrain type and recalculate its yields (`GameEngine.completeImprovement`,
`GameEngine.ts:8602-8617`). A tile carries at most *one* improvement, so
`pollution` (−1 food, −1 production, −1 trade) replaces whatever stood there
until a Settler clears it (`GameEngine.cleanPollution`).

== 5. City Center Bonuses

The city center tile (always worked) has special rules
(`EconomicManager.cityWorkedTiles`, `EconomicManager.ts:783`, applying
`CITY_CENTER_MIN` from `EconomicManager.ts:118`):

* *Minimum yields*: food ≥ 2, production ≥ 1, trade ≥ 1 (floors, not overrides)
* There is **no river bonus**: the live yield path gives a centre tile no extra
  trade for a river running through it (that rule survived only in the dead
  `src/game/City.ts`).

== 6. Worker Assignment System

=== How Workers Are Assigned

Every yield recompute (`EconomicManager.recomputeCityYields` →
`cityWorkedTiles`, `EconomicManager.ts:783-933`) picks the tiles:

. *Pool*: every tile of `cityTerritory()` (the 20-tile radius) that passes
  `canWorkTile()`.
. *Sort*: by raw total yield — `food + production + trade` — unless the city has
  a `governorWeights`, in which case the governor's own weights rank the same
  pool (`EconomicManager.ts:866-871`). There is no Civ1 4-tier
  `getTilePriority()` in the live engine.
. *Player pins*: tiles in `userAssignedTiles` are taken first, so a manual
  pick-up/drop survives the auto-assign (`EconomicManager.ts:892-899`).
. *Target*: `targetTiles = workers + 1` (`EconomicManager.ts:879`) — the city
  center is worked *for free* and every citizen then works *one extra* tile, so
  a size-1 city works *2* tiles (center + 1), a size-5 city 6.
. *Food floor*: before slots go to raw yield, the fill first buys enough food
  to out-eat the citizens plus the settlers the city supports
  (`MIN_CITY_FOOD_SURPLUS = 1`), ranked by food and then by score
  (`EconomicManager.ts:901-921`).
. *Fill*: the remaining slots go to the best-scoring tiles not yet chosen
  (`EconomicManager.ts:923`).

A citizen with no tile left is converted to an Entertainer (the idle-citizen
conversion in `cityWorkedTiles`), and when the city *shrinks*,
`fitCityToPopulation` / `fitWorkedTilesToPopulation`
(`EconomicManager.ts:998`, `:1017`) drop the worst tiles first.

=== Can a Tile Be Worked? (`EconomicManager.canWorkTile`, `EconomicManager.ts:769`)

A tile is workable if ALL of these are true:
* It is within the city radius (20 tiles + center)
* It is not the city center and not already worked by this city
* It is not claimed by *another city's* territory
* No *foreign* unit stands on it
* No Fisher Boat is actively fishing it — a netted ground belongs to the boat

== 7. Yield Accumulation

Each turn, `EconomicManager.recomputeCityYields(city)`
(`EconomicManager.ts:1051`) sums the yields of the assigned tiles, tile by tile
through `EconomicManager.tileYields` (`EconomicManager.ts:575`), with the city
center floored at 2F/1P/1T:

[source]
----
totalFood       = Σ food of the worked tiles
totalProduction = Σ production of the worked tiles + flat building/wonder shields
totalTrade      = Σ trade of the worked tiles + flat building trade + per-trade-square wonders
----

Buildings add *flat* bonuses, not multipliers: `buildingBonuses().trade` /
`.science` and `cityBuildingEffects().production`. The Granary has **no** food
multiplier — it declares `foodStorage: 2`, `growthBonus: 1`, `health: 1`
(`BuildingConstants.ts:57-68`).

Trade is split by the civ's *tax / science / luxury* rates in
`EconomicManager.cityOutputs` (`EconomicManager.ts:368-374`), not 50/50 (that
split survives only in the dead `src/game/City.ts`).

== 8. How Yields Drive City Economy

=== Food → Growth
* Food consumption = `population × citizenFoodDemand(population)`, where
   `citizenFoodDemand(p) = 2 + 0.2 × max(0, p − 5)` — the appetite rises with
   city size, so a large city is harder to keep fed than a small one
* Food surplus → stored in `foodStored` (`TurnManager.processCityGrowth`,
  `TurnManager.ts:1368-1375`)
* Growth threshold = `(population + 1) × 10`, held in `foodNeeded`
  (`TurnManager.ts:1373`)
* When `foodStored ≥ foodNeeded` → city grows (+1 population)
* Granary: retains 50% of stored food on growth; without it, food resets to 0
* Starvation: if `foodStored < 0` → population decreases, weakest tile unworked
* A tile occupied by a *foreign* unit is not worked at all

=== Production → Buildings & Units
* `productionProgress += production` each turn
* When progress ≥ item cost → item completes
* Excess production carries over to next item

=== Trade → Gold & Science
* Gold: used for unit maintenance, rushing production, diplomacy
* Science: used for technology research

== 9. Settlement Placement

=== Minimum City Distance

Defined as `MIN_CITY_CENTER_DISTANCE = 5` in `SettlementEvaluator.ts`:

* Two friendly cities must be at least *Chebyshev distance 5* apart.
* This guarantees their 5×5 workable radii *never overlap* — no tile competition.

=== Settlement Scoring (`SettlementEvaluator.findBestSettlementLocation()`)

The AI searches a *10×10 area* around the settler and scores each location:

. *Validity checks*: Not ocean, not mountains, no existing city, no blocking unit
. *Distance check*: Must be ≥ MIN_CITY_CENTER_DISTANCE from all friendly cities
. *5×5 area evaluation*: sums food/production/trade for all *25* tiles of the
  candidate's workable area, the inner ring (Chebyshev distance ≤ 1) counted in
  full and the outer ring at 0.5 (`SettlementEvaluator.evaluateArea`,
  `SettlementEvaluator.ts:137-201`)
. *Resource multiplier*: special resource tiles have their yields *doubled* —
  in this score only, never in the real yields
. *City proximity penalty*: tiles within reach of another city get penalized
  (−1 per overlapping friendly tile, −0.2 per enemy tile)
. *Water bonus*: +2 score if adjacent to ocean/sea
. *Weighted score*: `food × food_weight + production × shields_weight + trade × gold_weight + water_bonus`

=== Settler Decision Logic

* Settler evaluates its current tile score vs. best location in 10×10 window
* If current tile score is within `SETTLE_SCORE_THRESHOLD` (12 points) of best → found here
* If best location is beyond `MAX_SETTLE_WALK_DISTANCE` (4) → found here anyway
* Anti-oscillation: if settler bounces between two locations, it founds at current position

== 10. Site Evaluation for Existing Cities

The live engine has **no** per-city site score: `City.evaluateCitySite()` (with
`foodPotential` / `productionPotential` / `tradePotential` / `resourceScore` /
`overallScore`) lives only in `src/game/City.ts`, which nothing imports — it is
dead code, like the rest of that module.

The only site evaluation the game actually runs is the one for *new* cities,
`SettlementEvaluator` (section 9): a 25-tile area score with the strategy
weights, the resource doubling and the proximity penalty described there.

== 12. On-Screen Tile Highlights (Visual Feedback)

When a city is selected in-game, the map renderer (`src/game/rendering/MapRenderer.ts`) draws two visual layers to help the player understand tile ownership at a glance.

=== Layer 1 — Other cities' radii (red, dashed)

Before drawing the selected city's own radius, the renderer scans *every other city* and computes its 20-tile diamond. Any tile that overlaps with the selected city's radius is marked as *blocked* and drawn with:

* *Red fill* (`rgba(220, 50, 50, 0.18)`) — translucent red tint
* *Red dashed border* (`rgba(220, 50, 50, 0.85)`) — dashed strokes signal "claimed by another city"

=== Layer 2 — Selected city radius (green + gold)

The selected city's own 20-tile diamond is drawn *on top* of the red layer, with each tile coloured according to its work status:

[cols="1,1,1,2", options="header"]
|===
|Tile status |Fill |Border |Meaning

|*Worked* by a citizen
|`rgba(50, 200, 80, 0.28)` green
|`rgba(50, 200, 80, 0.9)` solid green
|Citizen is assigned here; tile yields contribute to city output

|*Unworked* (free in radius)
|`rgba(255, 214, 0, 0.12)` gold
|`rgba(255, 214, 0, 0.6)` solid gold
|Within city radius but no citizen assigned yet

|*Blocked* by another city
|(drawn red in layer 1)
|(drawn red in layer 1)
|This tile belongs to a different city's radius
|===

*City center* always gets a thick gold outline (`#ffe066`, 2× lineWidth) plus the green fill — it is always worked.
