= AI city stability: civil unrest, tile build-out and the settler pipeline
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

== Why this exists

A large AI city fell into *civil unrest* and stayed there. In this rules set
unhappiness is unbounded — `unhappiness == population`, there is no government
tolerance — so a size-12 city needs twelve happiness points, and a disordered
city has its tax, science *and* commerce zeroed. The buildings that would fix
it (temple, colosseum, cathedral) are exactly the ones `BuildingAnalyzer` sells
when they stop paying, so the durable levers are the ones that were missing:

* *roads* — the only source of trade, and trade is what the luxury rate turns
  into happiness;
* *irrigation* — the only source of the food a big city needs to grow;
* *specialists* — entertainers (happiness), taxmen (gold), scientists
  (science), which a built-up city can afford and a raw one cannot.

This document records what was wrong, what was changed, what the test runs
show, and what is still open.

== Diagnosis

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Finding |Where

|`luxuryNeedPct` credited up to four points of *hypothetical* entertainers the
governor had never hired (and often refused to, because of the food-headroom
gate). The city was unhappy, `anyCityProblem` said so, and the answer came out
`min(0, 30, …) = 0 %` luxury — so a disordered city got no relief from the
rates either.
|`AIEconomicManager.luxuryNeedPct`

|A one-city civ was allowed *two* improvements in its whole empire (the
`improvementBudget` floor was 2), so its fields stayed raw: no roads, no
trade, no luxury, unrest from size 4 on.
|`AIManager.improvementBudget`

|`pickWorksTileForCity` bounded only `dCol`, never `dRow`. The whole vertical
strip of `|dRow| ≤ 5` counted as "inside the city's area", so the works
programme handed out tiles five rows above the city — which the arrival side
(correctly) refused as outside the radius. 62 refusals, 47 of them idle
setttler turns, in a pinned 120-round game.
|`AIManager.pickWorksTileForCity`

|`worksArrival` compared `_aiWorksTarget` to `target`, and for a settler
`chooseAITarget` returns `_aiWorksTarget` verbatim — so it tested *"do I have a
works target?"* rather than *"have I arrived?"*. The branch fired on the very
turn the target was assigned, evaluated `chooseImprovementForSettler` at the
settler's current (mid-walk) position, found nothing, cleared the target and
skipped. **The settler never walked to its field at all.**
|`AIManager.runAITurn`

|The improvement budget was applied to *build-out* as well as decoration, so a
young empire stopped paving exactly when it needed the trade most.
|`AIManager.citiesNeedBuildOut` (new)

|`ensureProductionQueue` called `determineFallbackUnit`, which has never
existed. Every call threw (swallowed by a `try/catch`), so the AI never queued
a single follow-up item and cities sat with an empty queue.
|`AutoProduction.determineFallbackUnit` (new)

|Idle citizens — a city with more citizens than fields — were simply dropped
on the floor.
|`EconomicManager.cityWorkedTiles`
|===

== Changes

=== Happiness and rates

* `AIEconomicManager.luxuryNeedPct` now counts only specialists that *exist*,
  and includes the government's `happinessBonus`. An unhappy city gets a real
  luxury rate instead of 0 %.
* `AICityManager`:
** the entertainer cap scales with the city — `specialistCapForPopulation`
   gives 2 citizens up to size 8, then one more every three, capped at 5;
** that size-based staff unlocks only once half the city's workable area is
   improved (`SPECIALIST_UNLOCK_FRACTION`), because a raw city cannot spare
   the workers;
** the governor no longer demotes an entertainer unless the city has *two*
   happiness points of slack **and** a field to put the citizen on — the old
   rule flipped the city back to unhappy next turn and re-hired it, one citizen
   ping-ponging between the fields and the stage.

=== Tile build-out

* `citiesNeedBuildOut(civId)` — some own city's area is less than half
  improved. Build-out is *exempt* from the civ-wide improvement budget.
* The settler loop runs the works path when `prefersInfrastructureOverExpansion
  || hasMatureCity || citiesNeedBuildOut`, so a young empire paves before it
  founds instead of the other way round.
* `pickWorksTileForCity`:
** `inArea` now bounds **both** axes;
** roads are preferred on tiles the city already works when the city needs
    commerce (`cityNeedsCommerce`: unhappy *and* trade below unhappiness) —
    self-limiting, it stops as soon as trade covers the unhappiness;
** the margin ring (outside the radius) is only offered for irrigation, and
    `chooseImprovementForSettler` accepts it whenever the ditch can be dug
    rather than only when the *nearest* city happens to be food-short.
* `cityIsUnderDirectThreat` has exactly the two blockers the design calls for:
  an enemy standing **in the fields** (Chebyshev 2) and an assault within the
  last six rounds. The 8-tile `identifyThreatenedCities` defence signal was
  removed from it — it is true most of the time on a map with barbarians and it
  silenced the works programme completely.

=== Settler pipeline

* `worksArrival` tests the settler's own position.
* The works target is cleared when the block produces none, so a stale
  `_aiWorksTarget` pointing at a tile the same settler had just finished
  irrigating can no longer fire the arrival branch.
* `chooseAITarget` records `_aiWorksTarget` when it returns a trade-road tile —
  it used to hand the tile back without recording it, so the settler walked
  there and then held.
* `chooseImprovementForSettler(unit, targetIsGated)` — the threat re-test runs
  only when the settler chose the tile itself; a tile from the works programme
  already passed the threat test for *its* city, and re-testing it against the
  *nearest* city vetoed 117 work attempts per game.
* Every refusal is logged (`improve_reject:<reason>`), and a failed attempt on
  a works tile logs `works_fail` — the whole class of bug above was invisible
  before.

=== Economy

* `AutoProduction.determineFallbackUnit` implemented (it never existed).
* `evaluateGoldSpending`: the spendable amount now subtracts next turn's
  upkeep, so a flush treasury cannot be rushed down to `reserve + 15` and then
  go negative when upkeep is charged.

=== Idle citizens

* `EconomicManager.cityWorkedTiles`: a citizen with no field to work becomes an
  **Entertainer** automatically. The conversion lives in the engine, so it is
  automatic for every city; the governor may still staff *spare* citizens as
  Taxmen or Scientists, but a citizen with no field has no choice to make.

== Test runs

Three AI-vs-AI games, 300 rounds each, four civs, map `AI_VS_AI`, seeds
7/19/33, `Math.random` seeded with 4242 so two runs over the same code are the
same games. Driver: `scripts/run-ai-batch.mjs` (instrumented with improvement,
disband, specialist and per-action counters).

[cols="2,1,1,1,1,1"]
|===
|Run |Improvements built / game |Abandoned units / game |Largest city |Disorder (city-rounds) |`works_fail`

|Baseline (before this work) |5 |~12 |7 |2.7 % |117
|After the works pipeline |16 |11.7 |7 |1.1 % |0
|After `worksArrival` + margin fixes |26 |**7.7** |6 |**0 %** |**0
|===

What the numbers say:

* **The settler pipeline works now.** `works_fail` went from 117 per game to
  0; improvements went from 5 to 26 per game; improved tiles near own cities
  from 4 to 26.
* **Unrest is gone** in the final run: 0 of 90 city-round snapshots were in
  disorder.
* **Abandoned units dropped** from ~12 to 7.7 per game (and peaked at 77.7
  during an intermediate experiment) — *not zero yet*.
* **Cities do not get big.** The largest city in the final run was size 6,
  against 10 in an intermediate run.

=== Reflection — is this going in the right direction?

Partly. The two mechanics the request was about — *build the city out with
roads and irrigation* and *let a built-up city carry 2–5 specialists* — are
implemented and measurably working: improvements per game up 5×, the refusal
that blocked them is gone, unrest at 0 %, specialists scaling with size (the
cap simply is not reached yet because cities are not getting big enough).

But the run does **not** yet show "bigger cities over a longer period", and
the reason is not unrest:

* **Every civ in every seeded run ends with zero cities.** Barbarians hold
  3.3 cities per game, `CITY_CAPTURED` fires 8.7 times per game, and
  `UNIT_DEFEATED` 22.7. Cities are being taken off the AI faster than it can
  grow them.
* Because of that, the acceptance test `tests/idleUnits.test.ts` reads
  **59.8 %** idle settler turns against a 35 % bar. Diagnosing it turn by turn:
  **97 of the 205 idle settler turns belong to a civilisation that already has
  zero cities** — orphaned units that cannot be given an order at all. Excluding
  orphans the figure is ~28 %. The remaining `(no log, 0 moves, civ alive)`
  bucket (78 turns) is still unexplained and is the next thing to fix.

== Open issues / next steps

. **Barbarian pressure wipes the AI.** Until a civ survives 300 rounds with
  its cities intact, "handle bigger cities over a longer period" cannot be
  measured. This is the single biggest blocker.
. **7.7 abandoned units per game.** The disbander runs when the treasury is
  negative *within* a turn, which no snapshot ever catches. Both
  `sustainableUnits` models were tried with a safety margin; neither moved the
  number, so the deficit is coming from somewhere else (likely the emergency
  rate raise setting luxury to 0 and collapsing income).
. **78 idle settler turns** from a live civ with 0 moves and no log — the unit
  is processed, consumes its movement and produces no trace.
. `tests/idleUnits.test.ts` counts units of eliminated civs as idle. Whether
  that is the right definition is worth settling before tuning to it.
